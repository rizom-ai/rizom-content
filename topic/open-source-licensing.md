title: Open-Source and Self-Hosted Software Governance
---
A governance and product framework for keeping organizational-memory infrastructure open, self-hostable, portable, and resistant to proprietary capture while enabling sustainable commercial activity. Rizom's runtime, platform, and core repositories are licensed AGPL-3.0-only (since July 2026), supporting deployment by organizations with procurement, security, sovereignty, or operational requirements—not only users who prefer self-hosting. Portable Markdown data, complete export and re-import, documented ownership deeds, public repositories, security review, and a required Developer Certificate of Origin sign-off provide verifiable data sovereignty and clean intellectual-property records.

The surrounding technical assets—including the OAuth-enabled MCP server, plugin SDK, atproto contracts, theme interfaces, and deployment tooling—support genuine self-installation and partner extensibility. The published SDK, contract, site, theme, and UI packages are licensed Apache-2.0, enabling broad reuse and commercial plugins without surrendering stewardship of shared infrastructure. Commercial value comes from deployment, expertise, training, supported operations, certified practices, and managed hosting rather than restricted access to the runtime. A reusable operator playbook reduces dependence on one-person delivery and makes self-hosting a scalable product architecture and go-to-market model rather than merely a free tier.

Rizom B.V. holds the platform copyrights and the Rizom brand today; contributions are accepted under a Developer Certificate of Origin licensed to Rizom B.V. and its successors and assigns. The settled structure is an independent Dutch stichting—still to be founded—that will own the core intellectual property and the brand and govern them as an open commons, with a statutory purpose written for that stewardship and at least one board member independent of the commercial practice. On founding, copyright and brand transfer by deed, and the B.V. receives an exclusive commercial licence-back—perpetual, irrevocable, sublicensable, and surviving change of control—retaining its services practice, partner and certification registries, client relationships, hosted operations, and related revenue. This separates commons stewardship from commercial operations, giving investors exposure to services and ecosystem activity rather than ownership of the core infrastructure.

Trademark and brand governance complement software licensing through clearance searches, published usage rules, registry controls, approval requirements, and protection for the Rizom name, logo, product names, certification marks, domains, package names, and namespaces. Forks and modified versions should use distinct names and avoid misleading association. Together, these measures support client trust, partner innovation, migration between managed hosting and self-hosting, and durable freedom from vendor lock-in.
