---
title: Open-Source and Self-Hosted Software Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for keeping organizational-memory infrastructure open, self-hostable, portable, and resistant to proprietary capture while enabling sustainable commercial activity. Rizom’s core runtime, platform, and repositories use AGPL-3.0-only, with data stored in portable Markdown and supported by complete export and re-import. Organizations can deploy the platform on their own infrastructure or migrate away from managed hosting without vendor lock-in. The AT Protocol, OAuth-enabled MCP server, plugin SDK, deployment tooling, themes, and related interfaces support interoperability, self-installation, and partner extensibility, while selected SDK and integration packages may use permissive Apache-2.0 licensing to encourage commercial reuse.

Commercial value comes from managed hosting, deployment, training, operational support, certification, partner services, and expert implementation rather than restricting access to the runtime or self-hosting. A reusable operator playbook makes self-hosting a scalable product and go-to-market model. Data ownership, public repositories, security review, documented contribution and intellectual-property practices, and Developer Certificate of Origin sign-off provide verifiable sovereignty and clean provenance.

Governance separates the open commons from commercial operations. An independent Dutch stichting is intended to steward the platform’s core intellectual property and brand under a statutory open-commons purpose and independent board representation. The commercial B.V. operates engagements, client relationships, hosted services, the partner registry, and related business activities under an exclusive, durable licence from the stichting. A separate STAK may hold contributor-earned economic ownership once the first ownership grant is made, while a designated commons share—such as 25%—can fund open-source development, infrastructure, hosting, and stewardship. This structure supports commercial delivery and investment without concentrating ownership of the core infrastructure in the operating company.

Trademark clearance, usage rules, registry controls, and distinct naming for forks protect the brand while preserving the freedom to modify, deploy, and extend the platform. The resulting model aligns commercial incentives with services and ecosystem growth, while maintaining transparent stewardship, contributor participation, interoperability, and freedom from vendor lock-in.
