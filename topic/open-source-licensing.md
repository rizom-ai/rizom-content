---
title: Open Source Licensing
---
Rizom’s governance framework separates stewardship of the open-source commons from commercial execution. The core runtime and brains repository use AGPL-3.0, while the plugin SDK, AT Protocol contracts, and theme interfaces use Apache-2.0 to support broad reuse and commercial extensions. Repository metadata, README licensing notices, contribution terms, and a Developer Certificate of Origin should clarify contributor rights and preserve clean intellectual-property management through organizational succession.

Rizom B.V. may initially hold the copyrights and trademarks, with a planned transfer of core intellectual property and the Rizom brand to an independent foundation or stichting. The commercial BV retains value through a perpetual licence-back, certified practices, partner and service registries, client relationships, and service revenue; this licence-back is essential to prevent the IP transfer from leaving the BV commercially hollow. Equity investors therefore receive exposure to the commercial practice rather than ownership of the core commons.

Copyright and trademark rights operate alongside the software licences. Contributors and ecosystem participants may reference, modify, and extend the software subject to applicable licence and attribution requirements, while the Rizom name, logo, product names, wordmark, certification marks, domains, package names, and namespaces remain governed by published policies, registry controls, or approval. Forks and modified versions must use distinct names and avoid misleading association. This structure protects the software commons and brand integrity while enabling partners, service providers, and commercial participants to build on Rizom under clear legal and organizational terms.
