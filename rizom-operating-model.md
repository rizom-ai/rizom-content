# Rizom Operating Model

Canonical statement of how Rizom relates to the people who build and deliver it. Drafted 2026-08-06. Supersedes the 2025 Business Brief ([[RizomBusinessBrief]], now historical); the [[cococo]] deck remains the principles statement this model implements. The [[investor-one-pager]] and the financial projections derive from this note — where they conflict, this note wins.

## What Rizom is

Rizom is a collective that builds and operates open knowledge infrastructure (Rizom Brains) and delivers engagements on top of it. It is not a staffing platform and not a conventional services company: delivery capacity comes from the collective, compensation is distributive per engagement, and ownership is earned through sustained commitment rather than granted by employment.

## The three tiers

- **Community** — the open ecosystem: users, contributors, ambassadors. Public membership, no financial relationship.
- **Collective** — curated professionals who deliver engagements together. Compensated per engagement from the delivery share. Vote periodically on decisions about the collective's future.
- **Core** — members who bring client projects or monetizable products and carry entrepreneurial risk. Compensated from the acquisition share. Sustained commitment at this tier is the path to ownership.

## The split: 60 / 15 / 25

Every engagement's revenue divides three ways:

- **60% delivery** — to whoever delivers the work, individually or as a team.
- **15% acquisition** — to whoever sourced the engagement and carries the client relationship.
- **25% commons** — to the platform: open-source development, the employed infrastructure team, hosting of shared services, and stewardship.

Historical note: earlier documents stated this split with varying labels (team/infrastructure/core owners; delivery/acquisition/commons). This is the single canonical reading.

## Compensation and ownership are different things

Contribution is compensated immediately, per engagement, through the split. Ownership is not automatic and is not promised by contribution alone: it follows sustained commitment at Core, over time. When the first ownership grant is earned, contributor ownership will be held as certificaten via a STAK (stichting administratiekantoor) — economic rights without governance fragmentation — while investors and founders hold B.V. shares directly. The STAK is incorporated when the first grant happens, not before.

## The entities

- **Rizom B.V.** — the commercial operator: engagements, partner registry, client relationships, hosted operations. Today the sole entity; holds the platform copyrights and brand pending the stichting.
- **Commons stichting** (to be founded) — will own the core IP and the Rizom brand and steward them as an open commons, with the B.V. operating under an exclusive commercial licence-back. Detailed in the open-source-licensing topic.
- **STAK** (at first ownership grant) — holds contributor-earned economic ownership as certificaten.

Three bodies, three answers: the stichting answers who owns the commons, the B.V. answers who operates commercially, the STAK answers how contributors come to own the operator.

## The infrastructure team

A small employed team, paid from the commons share, keeps the platform running — employment rather than distribution because reliability work must not compete with engagement work for attention. Policy: start with one role (platform operations — the hosted fleet, releases, support); add a second (onboarding and developer experience) when the commons flow covers the first or funding closes the gap. Collective members may carry these roles on retainer before employment fixes them. R&D wage support (WBSO) applies to employed development roles.
