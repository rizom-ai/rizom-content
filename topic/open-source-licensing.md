---
title: Open-Source and Self-Hosted Software Governance
---
A governance and product framework for keeping organizational-memory infrastructure open, self-hostable, portable, and resistant to proprietary capture while enabling sustainable commercial activity. Rizom’s runtime, platform, and core repositories may use AGPL-3.0, supporting deployment by organizations with procurement, security, sovereignty, or operational requirements—not only users who prefer self-hosting. Portable Markdown data, complete export and re-import, documented ownership deeds, public repositories, contributor terms, security review, and a Developer Certificate of Origin should provide verifiable data sovereignty and clean intellectual-property records.

The surrounding technical assets—including the OAuth-enabled MCP server, plugin SDK, atproto contracts, theme interfaces, and deployment tooling—should support genuine self-installation and partner extensibility. Apache-2.0 licensing for interfaces and extension points can enable broad reuse and commercial plugins without surrendering stewardship of shared infrastructure. Commercial value should come from deployment, expertise, training, supported operations, certified practices, and potentially trained operators rather than restricted access to the runtime. A reusable operator playbook can reduce dependence on one-person delivery and make self-hosting a scalable product architecture and go-to-market model rather than merely a free tier.

Rizom B.V. may initially hold platform copyrights and trademarks, with core intellectual property and the Rizom brand ultimately transferred to an independent foundation or stichting governed as an open commons. The commercial BV would retain its services practice, partner and service registries, client relationships, hosted operations, related revenue, and a perpetual licence-back to operate on and support the platform. This separates commons stewardship from commercial operations, giving investors exposure to services and ecosystem activity rather than ownership of the core infrastructure.

Trademark and brand governance should complement software licensing through clearance searches, published usage rules, registry controls, approval requirements, and protection for the Rizom name, logo, product names, certification marks, domains, package names, and namespaces. Forks and modified versions should use distinct names and avoid misleading association. Together, these measures support client trust, partner innovation, migration between managed hosting and self-hosting, and durable freedom from vendor lock-in.
