---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A foundation established as a long-term legal steward, such as a stichting, governs certification, use of the certified mark, registry standards, and partner admission, standing, and delisting across a distributed service network. Certification expands delivery capacity through trusted external practitioners and begins with apprenticeship on a real paid engagement rather than an exam or standardized curriculum. Practitioners use capability tags—such as facilitation, domain expertise, implementation, and deployment—and may progress from candidate to active to steward; dormancy reduces visibility rather than causing automatic expiration. A public, machine-readable registry records partner status, stewardship, delivery evidence, client accountability, completed engagements, and compatibility, with ongoing participation required to maintain standing. Certification grants participation at the Collective level within the Community–Collective–Core structure but does not confer Core status or ownership, which remain separate, earned invitations. The commercial practice participates as a partner under the same rules as external practitioners. Transparent pricing bands and a role-based economic model allocate revenue among delivery, client origination, risk, and commons stewardship, including support for platform development and foundation operations. Together, these mechanisms establish accountable participation, distinguish certification from ownership, and enable transparent coordination and sustainable growth.
