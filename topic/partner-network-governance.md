---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A durable partner-certification model governs eligibility, admission, participation, accountability, stewardship, and standing across a distributed service network. An independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification marks, methodology, partner agreements, public policy, pricing and registry information, engagement records, and fair delisting procedures. The public, machine-readable registry is authoritative: only currently listed active partners may use certified marks; candidates may identify themselves only as candidates during an active engagement, and mark rights end when status lapses. Disputes follow documented procedures with written reasons, a response or remedy period, and board appeal. Governance patterns may draw on established registry and approval systems, while a formal partner agreement should be finalized through legal review and validated through an initial partner and co-delivery engagement.

Certification begins through apprenticeship on a real paid engagement rather than an exam. Reusable eligibility criteria may incorporate relevant participation standards from comparable programs. Partners may hold capability tags and progress from candidate to active to steward. In return for access to the mark, registry, methodology, delivery tools, co-delivery and referral opportunities, partners deliver under published standards, uphold client commitments to portability and transparency, protect the marks, contribute evidence and learning to the commons, and remit an agreed commons share. Active standing depends on ongoing contribution and published expectations, with reduced activity potentially lowering visibility without automatic termination. Public claims, policies, pricing, registry data, and case studies should be verifiable before launch.

Certification does not create employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or Core status. Partners retain their independent businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted open-source platform access if they leave. Transparent registry evidence, published expectations, formal agreements, and clear governance preserve trust for partners and clients, while detailed thresholds, economic terms, eligibility requirements, and delisting standards remain subject to governance.
