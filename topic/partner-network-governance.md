---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A durable governance framework for certifying, admitting, supporting, evaluating, and, when necessary, delisting partners across a distributed service network. It establishes eligibility rules, formal partner agreements, participation standards, engagement records, accountability, and a public, machine-readable registry that serves as the authoritative source of partner status. Only active listed partners may use certification marks; candidates may identify themselves as candidates during an active engagement, and mark rights end when status lapses.

Certification is demonstrated through apprenticeship on a real paid engagement, with an initial partner or co-delivery engagement serving as a proof of concept for the broader network model rather than relying solely on an exam or public announcement. Partners may hold capability tags and progress from candidate to active to steward. In exchange for access to the marks, registry, methodology, delivery tools, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities, they uphold published delivery, transparency, portability, client-commitment, evidence-sharing, and brand-protection standards and remit any agreed commons share. Ongoing contribution and published expectations determine standing; reduced activity may reduce visibility without automatically ending participation.

An independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification methodology, agreements, public policies, pricing and registry information, engagement records, and fair delisting. Disputes follow documented procedures with written reasons, an opportunity to respond or remedy, and board appeal. The framework should be validated through legal review and informed by established registry and approval systems. Certification does not create employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or Core status: partners retain their independent businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted open-source platform access if they leave. Transparent registry evidence, verifiable public claims, formal agreements, and clear governance preserve trust for both partners and clients, while detailed thresholds, economic terms, eligibility requirements, and delisting standards remain subject to governance.
