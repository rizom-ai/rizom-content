---
title: Open-Source and Self-Hosted Software Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for keeping organizational-memory infrastructure open, self-hostable, portable, and resistant to proprietary capture while enabling sustainable commercial activity. Rizom’s core runtime, platform, and repositories use AGPL-3.0-only, with knowledge stored in portable Markdown and supported by complete export and re-import. Users retain ownership of their data and can deploy the platform independently, migrate away from managed hosting, or use managed hosting as an optional convenience. Interoperability through the AT Protocol, OAuth-enabled MCP server, plugin SDK, deployment tooling, themes, and related interfaces supports self-installation and partner extensibility; selected SDK and integration packages may use Apache-2.0 to encourage commercial reuse.

Commercial value comes from hosting, deployment, training, operational support, certification, partner services, and expert implementation rather than restricting access to the runtime or self-hosting. Public repositories, security review, documented contribution and intellectual-property practices, and Developer Certificate of Origin sign-off provide transparent provenance and accountability.

Governance separates the open commons from commercial operations. An independent Dutch stichting is intended to steward the platform’s core intellectual property and brand under an open-commons purpose with independent board representation. Rizom B.V. would operate client engagements, hosted services, partner activities, and related commercial operations under an exclusive, durable licence from the stichting. A separate STAK may hold contributor-earned economic ownership, while a designated commons share can fund open-source development, infrastructure, hosting, and foundation stewardship. Trademark rules, registry controls, and distinct naming for forks protect the brand without limiting the freedom to modify, deploy, and extend the platform. This model connects licensing, institutional ownership, contributor participation, and commercial sustainability while preserving data ownership, portability, interoperability, and freedom from vendor lock-in.
