---
title: Driver-Based Financial Modeling
---
Rizom’s 2026–2028 financial model uses explicit operating drivers rather than conventional forecasts. Conservative, base, and growth scenarios link pilot conversion, new engagements, retainers, pricing, costs, partner certification and remits, hosted brains, engagement conversion, and hiring timing to revenue, cash flow, and profitability. Hiring is gated by cash-flow thresholds, clarifying when personnel costs can be supported by commons flow; the conservative case still shows an early cash trough. Revenue is separated across engagements, partner activity, hosting, and commons allocations. The model serves as the canonical source for financial tables and should be regenerated when assumptions change. Sensitivity analysis identifies retainer churn, pilot delays, and the unproven pace of partner adoption as the most consequential variables, supporting decisions about capital timing and operational commitments while making key assumptions visible.
