# Rizom — The Commons Compounds

*Companion to [[investor-one-pager]] (story and ask) and [[rizom-financial-projections-20262028]] (numbers). Why the 25% is not a cost — it is where the long-term value accumulates, and why the B.V. is the only company positioned to harvest it. ## The mechanism

Every Rizom engagement splits 60% delivery, 15% acquisition, 25% commons. Most companies would book that 25% as a licence cost. In Rizom's structure it is an asset line: the commons share flows to an independent foundation that converts it — together with money only foundations can access — into infrastructure that never appears on a competitor's balance sheet and never has to be bought back.

## Three assets, in sequence

**The platform (live today).** Open source under AGPL, self-hostable, growing with every engagement. The foundation stewards it; the B.V. holds the exclusive, perpetual commercial licence — surviving any change of control.

**The programs (from 2027).** The foundation runs an open third space for the ecosystem: certification trainings, partner days, community cohorts, pilot showcases. The prototype is already designed — a funded program of pop-up institute editions developed with the Institute of Network Cultures. Programs are financed by the commons remit and by cultural and digital-autonomy funds that are open to foundations and closed to companies. Every program produces what the B.V. sells: certified partners, reference cases, hosting leads.

**The house (on evidence).** Cities everywhere reserve property for entities that provably serve the public: broedplaatsen in the Netherlands, community asset transfers in the UK, land trusts in the US, social-innovation spaces in Taiwan. The constant is the form — mission-locked foundations qualify for premises companies never touch. A foundation with a track record of public programming can claim that standing in any city where the ecosystem takes root. The program is itinerant by design; where it keeps returning, a house follows. Amsterdam and Taipei are simply where the ecosystem lives first.

## What this means for your investment

**Your capital never pays for infrastructure.** The commons remit, grants, and foundation-only property arrangements carry the platform, the programs, and eventually the walls — in every geography the same way. Money invested in the B.V. buys commercial capacity exclusively: sales, delivery, partner growth.

**The moat cannot be copied, and it multiplies.** A competitor can match features and undercut prices. It cannot match an ecosystem whose infrastructure is held by entities that cannot be bought — and each new city the partner network reaches is a place that moat can be planted again, on civic terms closed to companies.

**The flywheel becomes visible.** Network effects are the least believable claim in any pitch — until you can stand in them. Partners certified in the foundation's programs, pilots showcased at its editions, communities gathering on infrastructure they own, from Rotterdam to Taipei: by the institutional round, this is something an investor walks through, not a slide.

## The tests we hold ourselves to

- The first foundation program funded and run within twelve months of founding.
- Certification and reference cases demonstrably flowing from programs to B.V. revenue.
- Premises pursued only when the commons flow carries them — the house follows the program, never precedes it.
