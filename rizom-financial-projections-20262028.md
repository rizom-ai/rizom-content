# Rizom Financial Projections 2026–2028

Derived from [[rizom-operating-model]] (canonical split and policies) and [[investor-one-pager]] (pricing). Updated 2026-08-06. All amounts EUR thousands. These are driver-based scenarios, not forecasts. All figures are generated by the interactive model (local file rizom-financial-model.html, next to the one-pager PDFs; also published as a private artifact) — the model is canonical; regenerate these tables from it after any driver change.

## Assumptions

**Pricing** (from the one-pager): Knowledge Session 5; early-adopter build 15, retainer 4/mo; standard build 35, retainer 7/mo. New engagements price at standard from mid-2027.

**Split mechanics** (from the operating model): every engagement divides 60 delivery / 15 acquisition / 25 commons. The B.V. books the 25% commons on all system revenue, the 15% acquisition share on engagements it sources, and the 60% delivery share only where founder/employees deliver. As collective members take over delivery their 60% leaves the B.V. — modelled as a rising co-delivery share (0% → 30% → 50% in base).

**Partner economics** (from the one-pager): a modestly active partner generates ~238/yr system revenue → ~60/yr commons remit, plus a 10 one-off certification fee. Unproven until the first partner certifies — the model's main risk.

**Hosting**: 300 EUR/brain/mo average, 30% COGS.

**Costs**: founder draw 60–80/yr by scenario; hire #1 platform-ops 90/yr and hire #2 onboarding/DX 95/yr, both net of WBSO, starting per the hire gates below; platform overhead ~15–18/yr; program/growth spend 10 (conservative) / 15–40 (base) / 50–130 (growth) per year from 2027.

## Capital plan: two rounds

No scenario needs money to survive: with hiring gated on cash flow, even conservative is cash-positive from 2027. Capital is staged by what it buys, not by need.

**Round 1 — friends & family, ~EUR 75k, now.** SAFE or convertible with cap/discount, converting into round 2 — no valuation set today. What it buys: insurance against pilot slip and retainer churn, and hire #1 roughly two quarters ahead of the commons-flow gate. On base-case execution it is never spent on survival.

**Round 2 — institutional/strategic, EUR 250–300k, on evidence (indicatively 2027 H1).** Raised once the base case is visibly tracking: pilots converted, the first-partner test underway, reference deployments public. Buys the growth scenario roughly 18 months early: hire #2 pull-forward, partner certification program, reference deployments and cohort operations, commercial capacity, buffer. Strategic investors preferred: the model's value concentrates in network effects an aligned investor can accelerate.

Staging the rounds also fixes the terms problem: family money converts on friendly mechanics now, and the institutional round prices on 2027 numbers instead of 2026 promises.

## Sensitivities (the three that matter)

1. **Retainer churn** — one sustained retainer slot fewer across the period takes base-2027 net from +112 to +59; two roughly erase it.
2. **Pilot slip** — a quarter's slip in the pilots pushes 2026 H2 negative in all scenarios; harmless while hires stay gated, expensive if hired ahead of it.
3. **Partner ramp** — nearly all 2028 upside is partner flow, and it is unproven. Test: a first certified partner within twelve months. If it fails, 2028 base degrades toward conservative (~+166) — sustainable, not compounding.

## Honesty notes

- The model is high-leverage by construction: partner remits and hosting margin carry almost no marginal cost. That is the model's promise and its untested claim — stated here once so the investor brief doesn't have to hedge every line.
- INC/node-zero costs sit inside program spend and are capped per the operating model (one brain unfunded); the cohort is grant-funded or doesn't happen at this scale.
- WBSO is assumed netted into hire costs; if not claimed, add ~25/yr per development hire.
- Certification fees are one-off by design (standing is continuous, no re-certification); the recurring partner number is the remit, not the fee.
