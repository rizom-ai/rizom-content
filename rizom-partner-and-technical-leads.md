Five people surfaced 2026-09-09 from a sweep of 219 saved LinkedIn posts. These are partnership, technical and hosting leads. They are **not investors** — the investor work is [[rizom-investor-longlist-v2]] against [[rizom-investor-target-profile]], and nothing here should be merged into it.

**No outreach performed. Relevance is a hypothesis drawn from what each person published, not a stated interest in Rizom.** Connection degrees are as shown in the saved-posts list on 2026-09-09 and may be stale.

## 1. Michael van Lier — Builders, Rotterdam (1st degree)

Founder and Managing Director. His post says the hardest part of his job is finding "the person running 1TB of pooled memory under their desk, two Mac Studios and one Thunderbolt cable", who read a model card end to end for fun, who ripped out the orchestration framework and wrote their own over a weekend because the abstraction was lying to them, who knows exactly why retrieval got worse after the corpus doubled, and who will defend an opinion about evals at dinner. He notes that people like that do not apply to things, so Builders stopped waiting to be found: they now publish their opportunity domains, and have rebuilt their programme as a ten-week Rotterdam batch organised around evidence instead of pitching.

Why it matters: the described profile is a close match, and the approach is inbound-shaped rather than cold. Keep two things separate — the relationship, and the batch itself, which is a dilutive programme and therefore a round decision belonging with `ai-startup-accelerators`.

## 2. Daan Weddepohl — Peerby (1st degree)

Founder and CEO. Argues that a personal assistant is what the wealthy have today and everyone gets next; that a website is a do-it-yourself service counter which people with an assistant simply stop visiting; and cites Cloudflare measurements that machines now browse the web more than humans do. His bet is that second-hand goods end up traded like stocks, with agents on both sides and an exchange in the middle, humans present only at the very start and end of a transaction. He states he intends to build that exchange and is recruiting a marketing and community co-creator.

Why it matters: the same delegation thesis as Rizom, approached from marketplaces rather than institutional memory. A Dutch founder arguing a public agent thesis with an open call for collaborators.

## 3. Dick Hardt (1st degree)

Creator of OAuth. Building AAuth, which he describes as the internet identity layer for agents and humans. Posted "Is MCP the new HTML?" after building the Hellō Admin MCP Server and realising he was no longer building an app for humans.

Why it matters: agent identity and the Model Context Protocol sit directly underneath Rizom's technical surface. Relevant to how a self-hosted brain authenticates agents, and to provenance and declared standing in the record-level trust work now aimed at NLnet Restack.
