---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for scaling an open platform through a certified network of independent practitioners. Partners retain their own businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access while using shared methodology, operator playbooks, delivery tools, brand, registry, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities to provide services under the platform’s name. The model expands implementation capacity beyond the core team while preserving interoperability, quality, consistency, practitioner independence, and client ownership without requiring proprietary software.

Certification is earned through apprenticeship and demonstrated capability in a real, paid client engagement, typically beginning with co-delivery before progression to active partner or steward status. This makes certification practical and evidence-based rather than purely theoretical, but it also means partner growth depends on deal flow, successful pilot sales, reference deployments, and institutional demand. The network therefore increases delivery capacity without removing the need to secure foundational commercial engagements.

The framework governs eligibility, agreements, capability tags, participation standards, engagement records, accountability, published commercial terms, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry records partner status; only active listed partners may use certification marks, while candidates may identify themselves as candidates during an active engagement. Certification creates no employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or core-team status. Partners must meet published delivery, transparency, portability, client-commitment, evidence-sharing, interoperability, and brand-protection standards and may remit an agreed share of engagement revenue to the commons, proposed at 25% where adopted.

The model preserves a free and open runtime while governing commercial activity through certification, mark rights, delivery quality, revenue sharing, and access to shared resources. Ongoing contribution and published expectations determine standing, with reduced activity potentially lowering visibility without automatically ending participation. An independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification methods, agreements, registry and pricing information, engagement records, and fair enforcement, including documented disputes, opportunities to remedy, and appeal. Detailed thresholds, economics, eligibility rules, revenue shares, price bands, and delisting standards remain subject to governance and legal review.
