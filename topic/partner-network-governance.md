---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for scaling an open platform through a certified network of independent practitioners. Partners retain their own businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access while delivering under the platform’s shared methodology, playbooks, tools, branding, public registry, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities. This expands implementation capacity and distribution beyond the core team without requiring proprietary software or creating employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, client ownership, or platform lock-in.

Certification is earned through apprenticeship and co-delivery on real paid engagements, enabling demonstrated capability, practice validation, and case-study development rather than relying solely on a one-time examination. A single Certified Practitioner track uses capability tags and public standing states such as candidate, active, and steward. Only active listed partners may use certification marks, while dormancy may reduce visibility without automatically ending participation. Network participation depends on successful delivery, reference deployments, deal flow, and institutional demand.

The framework governs eligibility, agreements, capability tags, engagement records, published commercial terms and price bands, delivery and interoperability standards, evidence sharing, client commitment, accountability, trademark use, revenue allocation, registry publication, disputes, remediation, appeals, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry communicates partner status and capabilities. Partners may remit an agreed share of engagement revenue to the commons—such as a proposed 25% where adopted—to fund platform stewardship. The foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification, registry and trademark rights, listing decisions, standards, and enforcement. Open-source licensing, such as AGPL or a comparable framework, helps prevent proprietary capture, while detailed economics and thresholds remain subject to governance and legal review.
