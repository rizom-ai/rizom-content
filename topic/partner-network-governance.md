---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for scaling an open platform through a certified network of independent practitioners. Partners use shared methodology, infrastructure, delivery tools, brand, registry, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities to provide services such as surveying, workshops, playbooks, pilots, and retainers. This extends implementation capacity beyond the core team while preserving interoperability, quality, consistency, practitioner independence, and client ownership without requiring proprietary software.

Certification is earned through demonstrated practice in real, paid client engagements, supported by apprenticeship and progression from candidate to active partner to steward. The framework defines eligibility, formal agreements, capability tags, participation standards, engagement records, accountability, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry is the authoritative record of status: only active listed partners may use certification marks, while candidates may identify themselves as candidates during an active engagement. Mark rights end when status lapses.

Partners retain their independent businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access to the platform. Certification creates no employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or core-team status. In return, partners uphold published delivery, transparency, portability, client-commitment, evidence-sharing, interoperability, and brand-protection standards and contribute an agreed share of engagement revenue to the commons—proposed at 25% where adopted. The network’s durability depends on sufficient commercial value from certification, registry participation, brand use, referrals, and access to shared tools and methodology.

Ongoing contribution and published expectations determine standing; reduced activity may lower visibility without automatically ending participation. An independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification methods, agreements, public policies, registry and pricing information, engagement records, and fair enforcement. Disputes require documented procedures, written reasons, an opportunity to respond or remedy, and board appeal. Detailed thresholds, economics, eligibility rules, revenue shares, and delisting standards remain subject to governance and legal review.
