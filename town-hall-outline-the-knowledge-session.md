# Town Hall Outline — The Knowledge Session

*Internal. Speaker outline for the Rizom Town Hall of 9 September 2026, 60 minutes on Discord voice. Companion notes: [[tms-services-plan]], [[the-knowledge-session-one-pager]].*

## 0. Open (5 min)

Why this hour.
- The Knowledge Session is what pays for the next months. One session roughly covers a month. The first one needs to close in about eight weeks.
- It funds the year; it is not the year. Product and pilots stay the priority. This is the smallest thing a warm contact can say yes to.
- Two things today: how it works, and how each of you can help find the first one.

## 1. The reframe (5 min)

- Every organisation runs on knowledge that lives in people: who knows what, whose judgment settles which questions, how information actually moves.
- Nobody writes it down because it always worked.
- It stops working when a key person leaves, a team grows fast, or an AI tool is asked to do a job nobody ever specified.
- AI didn't break the knowledge system. It exposed that there never was one.
- That's why this can be bought on an AI budget while fixing an organisational problem.

## 2. How it runs (15 min)

Three steps, three weeks, fixed price EUR 5,000.

**Interviews** — six to ten people, one to one, 45 minutes.
- Two questions: what do you know that nobody else does, and what breaks if you're not here tomorrow.

**Workshop** — half a day with the team, on site or online.
- The team draws its own map: who knows what, whose judgment counts where, how information moves. Everyone sees the same picture, usually for the first time.

**Playbook** — the map as it is today, plus three to five interventions ranked by cost and impact.
- Three dimensions: expertise, trust, coordination.

What the buyer gives: six to ten names and half a day.
What the buyer keeps: a document. Nothing to install, no dependency on us. If they build later, the playbook is the spec, the fee counts toward it, and what gets built is open source on their own infrastructure.

The method: transactive memory systems, forty years of research on how teams share knowledge without everyone knowing everything. Three dimensions: who knows what, whose judgment is trusted, how it moves. High-performing teams score above 70% on TMS measures; most teams start well below. The gap is structural, not personal. AI is a new team member with none of the three.

## 3. How you can be useful (15 min)

**Who we're looking for.** Not engineering. Whoever owns how the work flows: a director, head of operations, CTO, COO, programme lead. Institutions (universities, libraries, museums, public bodies) and companies where a small team carries a lot of knowledge.

**The triggers.** Listen for one of these in your own network:
- A key person resigned or is about to.
- A reorg, merger, or rapid hiring.
- An AI rollout that underdelivered, or an AI agent that doesn't work reliably.

**What you say.** Two sentences, no more:
- "Rizom does a three-week diagnostic of who knows what in your organisation and what breaks when they leave. Fixed price, you keep the report, no dependency."
- Then: "Want me to connect you with Jan Hein?"

**What you don't do.** Don't pitch. Don't explain the method. Don't promise anything about price or timing. You connect; I take it from there.

**How an intro works.**
- You send one short message to both of us, or forward the one-pager and copy me.
- I reply within a day and take the conversation.
- You hear back from me what happened.

**What I need from you after today.** A name, not a category. "A university" is nothing. "The head of the library at X, who lost her deputy in June" is a lead.

## 4. Names round (15 min)

Go round the room. Each person names one person or organisation matching a trigger, and says which trigger. I write them down. No discussion, no evaluation.

If someone has none today, that's fine. They say who they'll ask this week.

Close the round by reading the list back.

## 5. Close (5 min)

- The one-pager is in the brain as a shared note. Forward it, don't rewrite it.
- The list from today goes into the rizom-ai brain. I follow up on each name and report back.
- Next town hall: where each conversation stands, and what we learned about the pitch.
