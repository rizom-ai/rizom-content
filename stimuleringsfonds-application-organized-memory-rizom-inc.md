## What this project is

Twenty years ago the question of *organized networks* was posed from within Dutch internet culture: how does loose association become durable form without hardening into institution? It could not be answered then, because there was nothing to organize on except the platforms.

The question is now urgent rather than theoretical. Institutions that housed collective intellectual life are shedding it. ⚠ [INC's own situation — the community testing whether it can persist once its institution is gone — stated in INC's terms, from the working document.] What such a community loses is not primarily funding or a building: it is memory — the accumulated knowledge of who thought what, what was tried, what connects to what. Platforms offer to hold that memory, centralise it, and rent it back.

This project runs the experiment the 2006 question implied: **can a community organize itself on its own memory?** Every participant keeps their knowledge where they make it — personal, portable, owned. The project builds and studies the practice layer on top: how members' memories find each other, cite each other by name, and compose a collective memory that no platform holds and no institution needs to guarantee.

Rizom brings the infrastructure practice and the research frame; INC brings the community, the twenty-year intellectual lineage of the question, and the editorial practice to publish what is learned.

## Why this belongs in Digitale cultuur

This is internet culture reflecting on its own conditions of survival — research and reflection on network culture, carried out *as* network culture. The organized-networks question is part of the Dutch digital-culture canon; this project returns to it with the means to finally test it, and publishes the results in the field's own forms: essays, a publication, public program.

## What we will do (12 months)

1. **The experiment.** A working group of ⚠ [n] participants from the INC community each keep a personal, self-hosted knowledge environment; a shared group memory grows out of what they choose to connect. Not a pilot of a product — a study of what changes for a group when its members' memories can find and cite each other with no platform in between.
2. **The observation.** Documented throughout: what forms, what fails, where the group's memory exceeds any individual's, where organization by memory differs from organization by platform.
3. **The reflection.** ⚠ [INC-edited] publication bringing the findings back to the organized-networks debate: essays from participants, the observational record, and a concluding account of whether — and how — memory can be the durable form the 2006 question was looking for.
4. **The public program.** Two public events (Amsterdam/Rotterdam) presenting the experiment and its findings to the field.
