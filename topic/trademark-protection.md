---
title: Trademark Protection
---
A practical, staged framework for protecting the RIZOM word mark in the European Union and supporting an international partner ecosystem. Begin with clearance searches across TMview, EUIPO eSearch, BOIP, company registers, and broader web sources, focusing on potentially conflicting marks such as Rizom-Lab and RIZOMUV, particularly in software-related fields. A plain word-mark filing, initially owned by Rizom B.V., offers broad protection across visual presentations and can later be assigned to a stichting. Given the brand’s international scope, an EU trade mark application is generally preferable to Benelux-only protection; a Benelux filing may first be used to establish a Paris Convention priority date if the EU application follows within six months. The specification should cover only genuinely used or intended goods and services, potentially including software, AI and knowledge-management tools, SaaS, software development, business consultancy, education, and workshops in Nice classes 9, 35, 41, and 42. Conflict exposure can be reduced by narrowing software-related terms, excluding areas such as 3D graphics, computer graphics, and UV mapping where appropriate, using goods-and-services carve-outs, negotiating coexistence agreements, adopting fallback composite branding, and retaining less-contested consulting or training services. The strategy should also address opposition risks and deadlines, registration mechanics, the five-year genuine-use requirement, assignment and ownership-recording procedures, renewal obligations, and international expansion through the Madrid Protocol.
