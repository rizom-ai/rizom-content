---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for scaling an open platform through a certified network of independent practitioners. Partners retain their own businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access while using shared methodology, delivery tools, brand, registry, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities to provide services such as surveying, workshops, playbooks, pilots, and retainers. The model expands implementation capacity beyond the core team while preserving interoperability, quality, consistency, practitioner independence, and client ownership without requiring proprietary software.

Certification is earned through apprenticeship and demonstrated practice in a real, paid client engagement rather than through a purely theoretical curriculum. Candidates progress from co-delivery to active partner and, where appropriate, steward status. This approach strengthens delivery capacity but remains dependent on deal flow, reference deployments, sales, and a clear delivery model because candidates need live engagements in which to qualify.

The framework defines eligibility, formal agreements, capability tags, participation standards, engagement records, accountability, published price bands or other binding commercial terms, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry is the authoritative record of status: only active listed partners may use certification marks, while candidates may identify themselves as candidates during an active engagement. Mark rights end when status lapses.

Certification creates no employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or core-team status. Partners must uphold published delivery, transparency, portability, client-commitment, evidence-sharing, interoperability, and brand-protection standards and contribute an agreed share of engagement revenue to the commons—proposed at 25% where adopted. The partner model preserves a free runtime while governing use of the mark, delivery quality, commercial participation, and access to shared resources. Its durability depends on sufficient value from certification, registry participation, brand use, referrals, methodology, and delivery tools.

Ongoing contribution and published expectations determine standing; reduced activity may lower visibility without automatically ending participation. An independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification methods, agreements, public policies, registry and pricing information, engagement records, and fair enforcement. Disputes require documented procedures, written reasons, an opportunity to respond or remedy, and board appeal. Detailed thresholds, economics, eligibility rules, revenue shares, price bands, and delisting standards remain subject to governance and legal review.
