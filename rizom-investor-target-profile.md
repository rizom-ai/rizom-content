# Rizom — investor target profile

*Working profile established with the founder on 7 September 2026 through a sequence of yes/no questions. This records fundraising preferences, not authorisation to enter transactions or agreed legal terms.*

*Linked notes: [[rizom-round-budget-ask]] (current round budget and ask) and [[fundraising-strategy-and-decisions]] (fundraising strategy and decisions). Those notes are referenced only; they are not rewritten here.*

## Core profile

**An early-stage, growth-and-exit-oriented investor in Rizom's commercial operator, willing to accept independent foundation ownership of the core platform IP and durable funding rights for the commons.**

Conventional venture investors belong in the search. Do not restrict the list to patient capital, impact funds or dividend-oriented investors. Open-source experience is a useful signal, not proof of acceptance of the foundation/operator arrangement.

## Founder-confirmed preferences

| Topic | Position |
|---|---|
| Sustainable business without a sale | Would count as success. |
| Sale of the commercial operator | Acceptable and described as ideal, provided the foundation remains independent and the commons stays open. |
| Investor exit expectations | Comfortable with a fund expecting active pursuit of a sale in roughly 7–10 years. This is not a guaranteed exit or acceptance of particular forced-sale provisions. |
| Further funding | Willing to raise additional equity and accept dilution to accelerate growth. |
| Voting control | Willing eventually to lose majority voting control of the commercial operator, provided foundation independence and commons protections remain intact. Specific governance terms remain to be assessed. |
| Commercial risk | Willing to accept a higher risk of operator failure in exchange for a credible chance to build a much larger business. |
| Core IP ownership | Would reject an otherwise attractive investment requiring the operator, rather than the independent foundation, to own the core platform IP. |
| Commons funding | Durable commons funding is non-negotiable. The current model allocates 25%. The founder accepts review jointly with the independent foundation if actual transaction evidence shows the percentage is preventing adoption. This is not permission for an investor or operator board to cut funding unilaterally. |
| Founder location | Willing to relocate if funding requires it. Destination, costs and conditions are not yet agreed. |
| Corporate jurisdiction | Open to a foreign parent or another appropriate structure; the Dutch B.V. can be temporary. Foundation and commons protections must survive. No restructuring has been authorised or implemented. |

## Current round baseline

From local `investor-brief-composition.md` and the revised `round-budget-and-ask.md` (see [[rizom-round-budget-ask]]):

- €500k SAFE/convertible proposal: €400k at close and €100k at the composition milestone.
- Pre-revenue in the current brief, with a working platform and commercial proposals.
- Investors fund the commercial operator, not ownership of the commons.
- The two-tranche mechanics, instrument, cap, discount and conversion outcomes require negotiation and legal work; these questions did not settle them.

## Implications for investor sourcing

### Include

- Pre-seed investors comfortable with substantial technical and commercial uncertainty.
- Funds and operator angels capable of participating in a €500k round.
- Investors with relevant commercial-open-source, AI infrastructure, collaboration, knowledge systems or network/marketplace experience.
- International investors, including those that may require relocation or a different corporate jurisdiction, with those requirements recorded explicitly.
- Financially growth-oriented funds where ownership and commons compatibility can be qualified. Shared thematic interests are not evidence of legal-structure acceptance.

### Exclude or hold

- Investors whose business case requires the operator to own the core platform IP or weaken the independent commons.
- Investors requiring discretion to remove commons funding unilaterally.
- Funds requiring established revenue before investing: hold until their stage requirement is met.
- Investors whose minimum cheque cannot fit this round: hold/referral unless a genuine smaller-ticket route is verified. Do not assume the founder wants a larger raise.
- Revenue-repayment financing as the default instrument for this pre-revenue build: not the primary list, given pressure on operating cash. Alternative instruments have not been categorically rejected by the founder.

### Do not use as automatic exclusions

- Conventional venture return or exit expectations.
- Potential future dilution or loss of founder voting majority.
- Non-Dutch investor geography or corporate domicile requirements.
- Legitimate questions about the 25% calculation, transaction economics or an evidence-based foundation-approved review.
- Lack of an explicit impact-investing label.

## What remains to qualify per investor

1. Actual pre-revenue appetite and current deployment activity.
2. Cheque size, currency, lead/follow role and ability to fit the proposed round.
3. Acceptance of foundation-owned IP and the commercial rights the operator will actually hold.
4. Commons-funding obligations and governance requirements.
5. Portfolio overlap/conflicts and the relevant decision-maker.
6. Relocation/restructuring requirements, cost and timeline.
7. Evidence of relevant experience and a real contact/introduction route.

Ticket targets of €100k–€250k for funds and €25k–€100k for angels were assistant proposals, not confirmed founder constraints. Record published ticket ranges rather than treating those proposed ranges as hard filters.

## Assessment, not an additional founder commitment

The answers support conventional growth equity on protected commons, rather than a patient-capital-only strategy. The investment case still needs credible commercial defensibility: AGPL does not itself prevent competing commercial operators, and all network activity cannot automatically be counted as the B.V.'s revenue. The foundation/operator agreements must establish the rights and obligations an investor can underwrite.

Further funding should be assessed against evidence and dilution economics. Relocation or restructuring may be acceptable in principle but uneconomic for a €500k round. Willingness on either point is an option, not a reason to impose unnecessary complexity.
