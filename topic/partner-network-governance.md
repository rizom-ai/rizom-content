---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A durable partner-certification model governs admission, participation, accountability, and standing across a distributed service network. An independent foundation or similar legal steward oversees the certification marks, public machine-readable registry, methodology standards, partner agreements, and fair delisting procedures, including written reasons, a response period, and board appeal. Active standing is based on ongoing contribution and published expectations, with an activity window such as 18 months; reduced activity may lower visibility without automatically terminating participation.

Certified partners gain access to the certification mark, registry, current methodology and delivery tools, co-delivery support, referral opportunities, and collective membership. Certification begins through apprenticeship on a real paid engagement rather than an exam or standardized curriculum. Partners may use capability tags—such as facilitation, domain expertise, implementation, and deployment—and progress from candidate to active to steward. Certification does not create employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or Core status. Partners retain their independent businesses, capabilities, technical infrastructure, and self-hosted open-source platform access if they leave the program.

In return, partners deliver certified engagements under the published methodology, uphold client commitments to portability and transparency, protect the marks, maintain active standing, contribute delivery evidence and learning to the commons, and remit an agreed commons share. The registry records status, capabilities, stewardship, completed engagements, client accountability, compatibility, and other evidence needed for transparent coordination. Certification grants participation at the Collective level within a Community–Collective–Core structure; Core status and ownership remain separate, earned invitations. Transparent pricing bands and a role-based economic model may allocate revenue among delivery, client origination, risk, and commons stewardship. Detailed evidence thresholds, activity requirements, delisting standards, and economic terms remain subject to governance.
