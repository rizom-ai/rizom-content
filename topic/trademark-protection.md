title: Trademark Protection
---
A practical framework for protecting the RIZOM word mark in the European Union and supporting an international partner ecosystem. Before filing, conduct clearance searches across TMview, EUIPO, BOIP, company registers, and broader web sources, with particular attention to potentially conflicting marks such as Rizom-Lab and RIZOMUV, especially in software-related classes. Given the brand’s international scope, an EU trade mark application is generally preferable to Benelux-only protection; a Benelux filing may nevertheless be used first to establish a Paris Convention priority date, provided the EU application follows within six months. The specification should be limited to genuinely used or intended goods and services in Nice classes 9, 35, 41, and 42, including software, AI or knowledge-management tools, SaaS, software development, business consultancy, education, and workshops. Risk mitigation may include narrowing software-related specifications, using goods-and-services carve-outs, negotiating coexistence agreements, adopting fallback composite branding, and retaining less-contested consulting and training services. The strategy should also address opposition exposure and deadlines, registration mechanics, the five-year genuine-use requirement, assignment to a future stichting, recording ownership changes, renewal obligations, and international expansion through the Madrid Protocol.
