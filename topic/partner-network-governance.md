---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A durable governance and operating framework for scaling an open platform through a certified network of independent practitioners. Partners retain their own businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access while using shared methodology, operator playbooks, delivery tools, branding, registry services, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities to provide services under the platform’s name. The model expands implementation capacity beyond the core team while preserving interoperability, quality, consistency, practitioner independence, client ownership, and a free and open runtime without requiring proprietary software.

Certification uses a single Certified Practitioner track with capability tags rather than rigid sub-tiers. Entry is earned through apprenticeship and co-delivery on a real, paid client engagement, enabling continuous evaluation based on demonstrated capability rather than a one-time examination. Public standing progresses through candidate, active, and steward states. Dormancy may reduce visibility without automatically ending participation, while active listed partners alone may use certification marks; candidates may identify themselves as candidates during an active engagement. Certification does not create employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, ownership, permanent standing, or core-team status, and partner growth remains dependent on deal flow, successful pilots, reference deployments, and institutional demand.

The framework governs eligibility, capability tags, agreements, participation standards, engagement records, published commercial terms and price bands, accountability, revenue allocation, trademark controls, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry records status and capabilities. Partners must meet published standards for delivery quality, transparency, portability, client commitment, evidence sharing, interoperability, and brand protection, and may remit an agreed share of engagement revenue to the commons, such as a proposed 25% where adopted. The foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification methods, the registry, trademark rights, listing decisions, stewardship, pricing information, and enforcement through documented disputes, opportunities to remedy, and appeal. An AGPL or comparable open-source licensing framework helps prevent proprietary capture. Detailed thresholds, economics, eligibility rules, revenue shares, and delisting standards remain subject to governance and legal review.
