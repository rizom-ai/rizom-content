# Education Pilot — Commercial Proposal Template

**For an Austrian school as customer: developing and proving a repeatable offer in three independently valuable phases**

Template, September 2026. For use by KA21 as a certified partner with schools that commission the work from their own budget. Not for the first KA21 case, where KA21 is the launch client and commissions the phases itself under the Knowledge Session scope.

## Purpose

KA21 and Rizom propose to develop a school case through three independently valuable phases. The school is the customer. The aim is to validate that the school experiences enough value to pay for each phase, rather than treating the school as a demonstration site.

## The proposition is built around three decisions

### Decision 1: Knowledge Audit

**Is there a valuable organisational knowledge problem worth solving?**

A three-week audit of where critical staff knowledge sits, how information moves, where judgment is concentrated, what becomes difficult when key people are unavailable, and which interventions are worth pursuing. The school receives a practical playbook and a ranked set of interventions.

**Commercial basis:** €5,000 fixed price.

**Decision gate:** Stop, act on the playbook without software, or select one bounded opportunity for a first deployment.

### Decision 2: First Deployment

**Can a focused Rizom deployment materially improve one of those problems?**

One bounded use case is implemented and tested with real users. The objective is not to install a broad platform. It is to prove a measurable improvement against the problem and baseline identified in the audit. The deployment includes a Rizom Brain, the knowledge required for the selected use case, and one focused agent or workflow.

**Commercial basis:** the published Rizom price band for a six-month pilot, €35,000 including the audit, so €30,000 for the deployment. Scope, users, knowledge sources, responsibilities and success criteria are confirmed from the audit before commissioning. The €5,000 counts in full toward it.

**Decision gate:** Stop with a useful working solution, improve the deployment, or extend it.

### Decision 3: Extension

**Is the proven value strong enough to make Rizom part of the school's operating infrastructure?**

For larger institutions or school groups, the proven approach can be extended across additional teams, roles, knowledge domains, workflows, connectors and agents. This phase is shaped around the institution rather than sold as a standard package. Governance, permissions, knowledge stewardship, support and local delivery become part of the design.

**Commercial basis:** custom scope and price. A hosting and support retainer at the published band is available from Decision 2 onward for institutions that would rather not operate the deployment themselves.

**Decision gate:** Extend only where the institution sees continued value and is willing to fund it.

## Phase 1 in detail: Knowledge Audit

The Knowledge Audit stands on its own. It is not a prerequisite disguised as consulting and it does not oblige the school to buy technology afterwards. Its purpose is to give the school a clearer picture of its organisational knowledge risks and opportunities, while giving KA21 and Rizom enough evidence to decide whether a first deployment is justified.

### Before commissioning

1. KA21 introduces the opportunity to a school and confirms that the school is open to an exploratory conversation.
2. The school, KA21 and Rizom hold an exploratory conversation of about one hour. KA21 leads; Rizom supports where needed. The purpose is to understand the school's situation, not to sell a predetermined solution.
3. If there appears to be a useful problem and sufficient commitment, the school decides whether to commission the Knowledge Audit.

### Delivery over three weeks

1. **Preparation.** Half a day to align on the school context, interview approach, practical boundaries and what a useful implementation opportunity would look like.
2. **Staff interviews.** Five to eight individual conversations of about 45 minutes across one to two weeks, in German.
3. **Knowledge-mapping workshop.** Half a day with relevant staff to map critical knowledge, recurring questions, decision authority, information flows, friction and resilience risks.
4. **Synthesis and playbook.** A concise current-state map, three to five interventions ranked by expected value and effort, a recommended candidate use case, implementation prerequisites, and a baseline against which phase 2 can be evaluated.

### What the school provides

1. Five to eight staff members for individual interviews.
2. Relevant staff for a half-day workshop and a suitable room.
3. A sponsor who can help clarify priorities and make decisions.
4. No pupil data, system access or IT integration is required during the audit.

### What the school receives

1. A map of where important organisational knowledge sits today and where the school depends heavily on individual people.
2. A clearer view of how staff find information, where recurring questions emerge and where judgment or context is difficult to transfer.
3. Three to five practical interventions, including options that do not require software.
4. A recommended use case for phase 2, only if the evidence supports it.
5. A baseline and success criteria for the selected use case, so a deployment can be evaluated as an experiment rather than merely installed.
6. A playbook the school keeps and can act on whether or not it continues with KA21 or Rizom.

### Trust and boundaries

The audit examines organisational resilience and access to knowledge. It audits the organisation's memory, not its staff: nobody passes or fails. Individual comments are not attributed unnecessarily. The school controls how its information is used, and nothing specific to the school is reused elsewhere without agreement. Any later deployment is separately scoped and commissioned.

## Roles

| School | KA21 | Rizom |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer and budget owner | Owns the school relationship, local context and delivery | Owns the method and the platform |
| Provides staff access and decision sponsorship | Leads the audit and the customer relationship | Supports delivery by role as agreed; engineering on deployments |
| Decides whether each next phase creates enough value to fund | Sells and delivers under the Rizom name at the published bands | Stewards the shared platform and partner network |

## What each case should prove

1. A school recognises a material knowledge problem and is willing to pay to understand it better.
2. The audit produces insights and interventions the school considers useful even without a subsequent technology purchase.
3. At least one bounded use case has enough expected value, usable knowledge and internal ownership to justify a deployment.
4. The phase 2 outcome can be evaluated using observable success criteria.

## Next step

KA21 arranges the exploratory conversation. If the school sees enough value and is willing to commission the audit, the three-week phase begins. No commitment to phase 2 or phase 3 is created by commissioning phase 1.
