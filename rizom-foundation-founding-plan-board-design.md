# Rizom Foundation — Founding Plan & Board Design

Decided 2026-08-09 (working session, Jan Hein), ahead of the inaugural advisory-board meeting on Thursday 2026-08-13. Supplements [[rizom-operating-model]] (entities section) — on foundation governance, this note wins. Local documents in ~/Documents/stichting/: board brief (PDF, Rizom theme), inaugural agenda, statutes term-sheet, first-year docket.

## Naming
English documents say "the Rizom Foundation" throughout; the legal name in the deed is Stichting Rizom. Dutch terms stay out of English copy.

## Board design
- Jan Hein takes the **chair**. Founding board of three: chair plus two independent seats (secretary, treasurer), drawn from the advisory board. Statutes allow 3–5 members with an **independent majority guaranteed at all times**.
- **Reserved decisions** — decided by the independent members alone; the chair is statutorily conflicted, abstains, and has no casting vote: licence amendment or termination; any transfer or encumbrance of the platform IP or marks; certification, delisting or appeal decisions involving the B.V. or any member's interest; commons-remit disputes; any transaction between foundation and B.V., including grant-funded subcontracts.
- Precedent: the Ghost/Mozilla shape — the founder chairs the commons; the independent members hold every vote that touches the founder's company.
- Compensation: none at founding; the statutes stay permissive so the board can adopt its own compensation policy once the commons flow carries it.
- Succession (B.V.-side, not in the statutes): as revenue allows, the B.V. hires its own commercial lead and the founder's working centre moves fully to the commons. Framed as roadmap, not vacancy — the F&F round backs the founder.

## Founding
Before end of August 2026, by notarial deed, all costs carried by the B.V. The founding transfers execute as one package: copyright deed + trademark assignment + the exclusive perpetual licence-back (never one without the other), approved by the independent members alone — the first live use of the reserved-decisions article.
