# Close Kit — Knowledge Session

Track B from [[fundraising-strategy-and-decisions]] (2026-08-03): the founder runs scripted, warm-only, small-paid-first-step closes. This is the script. Drafted 2026-09-02. Product definition follows [[tms-services-plan]]; pricing follows [[fundraising-strategy-and-decisions]]; the offer sits inside [[rizom-operating-model]] and [[rizom-product-plan]]. A Dutch working copy is held locally (~/Documents/fundraising/close-kit.md).

## The clock

€0 in the BV. Monthly nut €5,200. Eight weeks from 3 August is 28 September. One Knowledge Session at €5,000 covers roughly a month; one session a month is break-even. Target: one signed session by 28 September. Not a pipeline — one.

## How this fits the warm outreach

The warm-network messages (held locally in ~/Documents/network-outreach/) contain no ask. Their job is to get a call. This kit is what happens on the call. The ask is spoken, about a priced product — never written into a message to a warm contact.

## 1. The offer, in three lengths

**One line.**
A Knowledge Session: in three weeks I map which knowledge lives in your people rather than in the company, and what breaks if one of them leaves tomorrow. Fixed price, five thousand euro, stands on its own.

**The paragraph** — for "how does that work."
Three steps. First I talk to six to ten people one-to-one — the fragility test: what do you know that nobody else does, and what goes wrong if you're not here tomorrow. Then half a day with the team where we draw the map together: who knows what, whose judgment settles which questions, how information actually moves. Then you get a playbook: that map as it is today, not as you'd like it, plus three to five interventions ranked by cost and impact. That's it. If you want to go further afterwards, the amount counts toward a pilot; if not, you have something that stands on its own.

**What they get** — for the outline and for your own certainty.
- Survey: 6–10 one-to-one interviews running the Fragility Test.
- Workshop: half a day in-house (≤15 people) or 90 minutes online.
- Playbook: the as-is map across the three TMS dimensions — Expertise Map, Trust Boundaries, Operating Playbook — plus 3–5 ranked interventions.
- Three weeks elapsed. €5,000 fixed. Credits toward a pilot build (€15k early-adopter). Nothing to install, nothing to buy afterwards.

## 2. Who it's for — and who it isn't

**Buyer.** Not engineering. COO, CTO, Head of Operations, or a programme director inside an institution — whoever owns how work actually flows.

**Triggers — listen for these; don't manufacture them.**
1. A key person resigning or about to.
2. A reorg, merger, or rapid hiring.
3. An AI rollout that underdelivered — the wedge. The line: *AI doesn't break your knowledge system. It exposes that you never had one.* Lets the session be bought on an AI budget while fixing an organisational problem.
4. Onboarding that keeps interrupting the same three people.
5. An AI agent that "sometimes works" and nobody trusts — the beachhead trigger ([[beachhead-scale-with-agents]], team decision 25 August). The problem is almost never the model; it's the context the agent lacks: rules, permissions, escalation paths, current knowledge. The session produces exactly that specification — the playbook becomes the spec for the context layer the two-week pilot then builds. Same session, same price, different second step. A buyer who pays for the map before any agent work is assumption #6 in [[assumptions-register-scale-with-agents]] tested in the cheapest possible way.

**Stop signals — end warmly, don't push.**
- No concrete pain; general curiosity about AI. Too early.
- The process itself is contested internally. A map can't fix a workflow nobody agrees on — that's an operating-model problem, and saying so is worth more than a sale.
- They want "AI search over our documents." Different product, different conversation.

## 3. The call — the arc, with the lines

Warm only. They already know you. You are not pitching; you are describing a thing you do and asking whether it fits. Every ask is a question about them with an easy yes.

**Open — nothing about you yet.** Whatever the message was about: their article, their project, the thing they posted. Two or three minutes of that, genuinely.

**Turn — one line, then stop.**
"I'm curious how that sits with you, actually." [pause]

**Listen for a trigger.** If one appears, name it back in their own words. If none appears after five minutes, this is a reconnection, not a sale — enjoy it and skip to §6. Don't force it.

**If the trigger is the agent** (trigger 5): qualify with the five questions from [[discovery-call-playbook-scale-with-agents]] — which workflow, where does it break or get corrected most, is the process itself well-defined internally, who owns the fix today, and what would "reliable enough" look like in numbers. If the process is contested, stop: a map can't repair a workflow nobody agrees on. Otherwise the session is the diagnosis and the pilot is the second step.

**Reframe — only if a trigger appeared.**
"What I keep seeing: the knowledge is in three people and nowhere else, and nobody wrote it down because it always worked. Until it doesn't."

**Describe — the one line from §1, then the price in the same breath.**
"I do a fixed session for exactly that. Three weeks, five thousand euro, stands on its own."

Say the number plainly and keep going. A hesitated number reads as need; a flat number reads as a product. No unprompted discount.

**Close 1 — the only close said out loud.**
"Shall I send you the outline? One page — you'll see exactly what you get."

That is the whole spoken close. It asks permission to send information. The real decision moves to paper (§4).

## 4. The written close — the outline email

Send within 24 hours of the call. One page. Price visible. One proposed date. The date is the close: yes to a date is a signed session; "not that week" is a negotiation about when, not whether.

Subject: Knowledge Session — outline

Hi [name],

Good to talk yesterday. As promised, the outline, so you can see exactly what you'd get.

**What it is.** Over three weeks I map which knowledge at [company] lives in people rather than in the organisation, and what breaks if one of them leaves.

**How it runs.**
1. Interviews — six to ten 45-minute conversations, one-to-one. The fragility test: what do you know that nobody else does.
2. Workshop — half a day with the team. We draw the map together: who knows what, whose judgment settles which questions, how information actually moves.
3. Playbook — that map as it is today, plus three to five interventions ranked by cost and impact.

**What you need.** Six to ten names and half a day in the calendar. That's it.

**Price.** €5,000 fixed. Stands on its own; if you go on to a pilot, it counts toward it.

**Proposal.** Interviews the week of [date], workshop on [date]. Does that work?

Jan Hein

## 5. Objections — the six that will come

One sentence each, then stop talking.

- **"Sounds like consulting."** One fixed-price session with a tangible result. Consulting is when I stay on afterwards — and I don't.
- **"We already have a wiki / Notion / Confluence."** That's where the documents are. This is about what isn't in them and why — who really knows, and what happens when that person is gone.
- **"Five thousand for a workshop?"** Not for a workshop — for eight to ten interviews, half a day, and a playbook. The workshop is the middle third.
- **"Can't we just try the software?"** You can — it's open source, it's on GitHub. But software solves nothing if you don't know what goes into it. That's exactly what the session produces.
- **"How is this different from [X]?"** I don't know what [X] does precisely, so I can't honestly say. What I do know: this is built on forty years of organisational psychology about how teams share knowledge, and the result is a map of your team, not a generic model.
- **"Not now."** Completely fine. Shall I check back in a month?

That last answer is a second close and the only follow-up needed.

## 6. After the call

- Outline within 24 hours. Every hour past that halves the yes.
- Log it in [[customer-discovery-log-scale-with-agents]] — currently empty. Who, what they said, trigger or none, decision, next step.
- One follow-up only if the outline goes quiet: day 10, one line, the proposed date moved by two weeks. Then stop.
- On "not now": calendar one month out. That is the entire cadence.

## 7. Delivery — what's built and what isn't

- Workshop — designed, ready. Half-day in-house and 90-minute online outlines exist in the yeehaa brain.
- Survey — interview protocol not yet written; derives directly from the workshop exercises; a day's work. Build on the first yes, before the first interview.
- Playbook — template not yet written; derives from the workshop canvas. Build during the interview week.

None of this is a reason not to sell the session — the KA21 draft already commits to delivering exactly this. It is a reason to sell one at a time.

The cap, from [[tms-services-plan]]: this funds the year; it is not the year. When a session competes for a week with a pilot milestone or a grant deadline, the pilot or grant wins.
