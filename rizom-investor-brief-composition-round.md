# Rizom — Investor Brief

*The composition round. Canonical text of the 2-page brief that supersedes [[investor-one-pager]]. Built on [[rizom-how-this-scales]] (the full scaling story) and [[rizom-round-budget-ask]] (the derived budget). Drafted 2026-08-31; restricted until reviewed, then replaces the shared brief.*

## Page 1 — Teams composed from what people actually know

*Rizom: composition infrastructure on an open substrate.*

Every organisation loses knowledge — the context behind decisions, the reasoning of a project, the expertise that leaves with people. The tools that promise to fix this are closed platforms, and whatever you put into them becomes the vendor's asset. Rizom Brains works differently: an open, self-hostable brain an organisation or a person fully owns — plain markdown on their own infrastructure, built on the AT Protocol, AGPL-licensed so nobody can enclose it.

### The claim

A brain holds two things about its owner. Living knowledge — what they know, kept current as a byproduct of real work, so it never rots the way knowledge bases do. And working patterns — how they decide, coordinate, collaborate. That second half is what nothing else captures: a CV records claims, LinkedIn records connections, a brain records demonstrated knowledge and demonstrated working style.

When brains can find each other, teams stop being assembled from org charts, credentials, and interviews — they get composed from evidence, across organisations and geographies. The methodology is transactive memory systems: forty years of research on why some teams outperform equally talented ones, extended by Rizom to teams that include AI. At scale, Rizom is not a knowledge-tools company. It is composition infrastructure, and its incumbent is not a competitor product — it is the org chart, the staffing agency, and the network of acquaintance.

### The mechanism

Two engines, different physics.

**Engine A — the practice — runs today and seeds the network.** We deploy and run brains for organisations: a €5k Knowledge Session that credits toward a pilot build (€35k), converting into a monthly retainer. Delivery comes from a collective compensated per engagement — capacity scales with the work, not with payroll. Every engagement funds operations and deploys brains: client teams, collective members, partners. Every deployment is a node.

**Engine B — composition — turns nodes into a network.** The TMS layer, built into the platform, composes teams from what the brains hold. Capacity and demand both scale with nodes: every person who joins brings what they can do and the work they know about.

### The evidence

We are pre-revenue, and we say so. What we have instead of revenue is evidence for each claim the model rests on:

- **Living memory works.** The platform is live and runs the company that makes it: rizom.ai is a brain running the platform it describes — talk to it. The founder's entire operation runs on brains, and releases ship continuously in the open at github.com/rizom-ai.
- **Composition demand exists.** Superconnectors, the founder's matchmaking network, grew past 1,000 members and expanded from the Netherlands to Japan with institutional partners including the City of Rotterdam and CitizenM — the exact behaviour Engine B automates, already done by hand at network scale.
- **The methodology produces outcomes.** At Varias, the company grew from 8 to 40 people in under two years on the founder's team methods, and the "virtual team member" AI tool built on them became the company's core IP. Behind that sit a Ph.D. and the TMS research tradition.
- **Organisations engage.** Three pilots in three sectors with tailored proposals in play, and a signed joint funding application with the Institute of Network Cultures. Converting this pipeline is not assumed in the plan — it is what the round's closing capacity exists to do.

## Page 2 — The machine, the commons, the deal

### The curve

Every composed engagement splits 60/15/25, and the split is the entity structure: 60% to the composed team — capacity scales with nodes, not payroll; 25% commons to the foundation that owns the open platform; 15% to the operator, Rizom B.V., that composed the team and found the work. Plus hosting for every node that prefers not to run infrastructure.

Illustrative arithmetic, not forecast — average composed engagement €50k, teams of three, an active node in one engagement a year, 40% of nodes hosted at €250/month:

- **1,000 active nodes** — the scale Superconnectors reached by hand — is ~€16M/yr of network throughput: ~€2.5M/yr operator take plus ~€1.2M hosting ARR for the BV, and ~€4M/yr commons funding a real platform team.
- **10,000 active nodes** is ~€165M/yr throughput: ~€25M operator take plus ~€12M hosting ARR.

The operator's costs do not scale with throughput — delivery is the network's 60%, the platform is commons-funded. Marketplace leverage on a substrate nobody can enclose.

### Why now

Three forces arrived together. Living memory became self-maintaining — knowledge capture always died on maintenance, and AI in the flow of daily work removes that cost. Teams already include AI — and TMS is the one body of team research that extends to members who aren't human. And work itself is deconstructing — fractional, project-based, cross-organisational — while the infrastructure for composing it still runs on credentials. Open protocols make the network commons instead of walled garden.

### The commons play

The 25% is not overhead — it is four moves:

1. **Supply acquisition at zero cost.** People contribute how they think and work only to a network nobody can take. A closed competitor must buy its supply side; ours joins for ownership.
2. **The substrate funds itself.** The commons share of all network throughput funds the platform team permanently — at the 1,000-node illustration, ~€4M/yr. This round is plausibly the last money that ever needs to fund the infrastructure. Ghost has run a version of this for a decade on one hosted product; Rizom's commons draws on the whole network's throughput.
3. **A second capital channel that never dilutes.** Public and grant funding (NLnet, Stimuleringsfonds, EU programmes) funds the commons because it is one. Investors fund the operator; the public funds the substrate; neither pays for the other.
4. **Non-paying nodes still compound the network.** Self-hosters, open-source contributors, researchers thicken the graph and the standard without a euro changing hands. A closed marketplace grows only when revenue grows; the commons grows the network everywhere.

### What equity buys

The mirror of the commons: investors own the sole commercial operator. The platform IP, the TMS layer, and the trademark move to an independent foundation (contributor sign-offs already make that transfer a single recorded deed); Rizom B.V. keeps a perpetual, irrevocable commercial licence that survives any change of control, the certification registry — the credibility dimension of TMS held as a commercial asset — the hosting relationship with every node, the client relationships, and 15% of everything the network composes.

### The ask

**€500k on a SAFE or convertible with cap and discount — no valuation set today.** Two tranches, derived bottom-up (the full budget is a companion document):

- **€400k at close** runs the machine for eighteen months. Every line does one of two things: raises node inflow — the founder full-time, a closer on the acquisition share, network seeding — or builds the composition layer: the engineering that makes Engine B real.
- **€100k released on one gate:** the first team composed across brains, earning its commons. It scales what that moment proves — second engineer, certification productized, hosted fleet.

Early money is first money into this round.

### Where this could fail

- **If engagements don't close**, the machine starves. The budget makes this measurable instead of vague: the closer thesis must show signal by month nine.
- **If no team composes across brains**, the gate never opens — tranche 2 is never spent, and the company continues as a self-funding practice on the Ghost path. The downside is a services business, not a zero.
- **If everyone self-hosts**, hosting revenue stays small — but self-hosted nodes still compound the network, and the operator take doesn't depend on hosting.
