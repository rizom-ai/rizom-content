---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for scaling deployment of an open platform through a certified network of independent practitioners. Partners use shared methodology, infrastructure, delivery tools, brand, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities to provide services such as surveying, workshops, playbooks, pilots, and retainers, extending implementation capacity beyond the core team while preserving interoperability, quality, consistency, and user ownership.

The framework defines eligibility, formal agreements, apprenticeship-based certification through real paid engagements, capability tags, progression from candidate to active partner to steward, participation standards, engagement records, accountability, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry serves as the authoritative record of partner status: only active listed partners may use certification marks, while candidates may identify themselves as candidates during an active engagement. Mark rights end when status lapses. Partners retain independent businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access to the open platform; certification creates no employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or core-team status.

In exchange, partners uphold published delivery, transparency, portability, client-commitment, evidence-sharing, interoperability, and brand-protection standards and remit any agreed commons share. Ongoing contribution and published expectations determine standing, although reduced activity may lower visibility without automatically ending participation. The registry, brand, and methodology may support the network’s commercial value proposition, while an independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification methods, agreements, public policies, registry and pricing information, engagement records, and fair enforcement. Disputes require documented procedures, written reasons, an opportunity to respond or remedy, and board appeal. Detailed thresholds, economics, eligibility rules, and delisting standards remain subject to governance and legal review.
