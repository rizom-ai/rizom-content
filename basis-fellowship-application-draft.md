**What's the best thing you've built, discovered, or pulled off?**

brains: an open-source platform where a person, a team, or a community owns its memory and the AI agent on top of it. Plain markdown on your own infrastructure, AGPL so nobody can lock it up, and the agents talk to each other directly over the open A2A protocol instead of through anyone's platform. I've written almost all of it myself, about 5,700 commits in fifteen months, and it isn't a demo: my website, newsletter, and email all run on my own brain, and pilots are live in an AI ecosystem, in cultural archives, and in farming cooperatives. Before that I taught philosophy at the University of Amsterdam for a decade. Pulling off the switch from arguing for institutions that can carry themselves to shipping the infrastructure they'd run on is the thing I'm proudest of.

**What do you believe that almost nobody else does?**

That AI doesn't break organizations. It exposes them. The companies it hollows out fastest were already treating their people as bots, executing decisions nobody felt they owned. Everyone debates whether the models are aligned; almost nobody asks whether the organizations deploying them ever had a working memory or a sense of who is accountable for what. AI is a stress test, and most of what it reveals was already broken. Which means the fix isn't better models. It's better institutions, and those can be built in code.
