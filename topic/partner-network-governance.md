---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A durable partner-certification model governs admission, participation, accountability, and standing across a distributed service network. An independent foundation or similar legal steward oversees the certified marks, registry standards, methodology, partner agreements, and fair delisting procedures. Certification expands delivery capacity through trusted external practitioners and begins with apprenticeship on a real paid engagement rather than an exam or standardized curriculum. Partners may use capability tags—such as facilitation, domain expertise, implementation, and deployment—and progress from candidate to active to steward; inactivity reduces visibility without automatic expiration, while standing remains contingent on ongoing contribution and adherence to published expectations.

Certified partners gain access to a public machine-readable registry, certified marks, current methodologies and delivery tools, co-delivery training, and potential referrals, but receive no guarantee of work, exclusivity, ownership, or Core status. They retain their independent businesses, capabilities, and infrastructure if they leave the program. In return, they deliver under the published methodology, protect the marks, uphold client commitments to portability and transparency, contribute delivery evidence and learning to the commons, and remit an agreed commons share. The registry records partner status, capabilities, stewardship, completed engagements, client accountability, compatibility, and other evidence needed for transparent coordination.

Certification grants participation at the Collective level within a Community–Collective–Core structure; Core status and ownership remain separate, earned invitations. The commercial practice participates under the same rules as external practitioners. Transparent pricing bands and a role-based economic model allocate revenue among delivery, client origination, risk, and commons stewardship, supporting platform development and foundation operations. The model establishes accountable participation without platform dependency while leaving detailed partner-agreement policies—such as evidence thresholds, delisting standards, and economic terms—to be finalized through governance.
