---
title: Organizational Knowledge Systems
---
Organizational knowledge systems are the infrastructure and practices used to capture, organize, govern, share, and coordinate critical knowledge across teams, platforms, practices, and foundations. Their health can be assessed through expertise, trust, and coordination, with particular attention to undocumented expertise, individual knowledge concentration, and the risks created when key people leave. Interviews and organizational mapping can reveal current knowledge flows, ownership, dependencies, and gaps rather than an aspirational future state. Knowledge ownership and maintenance responsibilities should remain explicit, especially when no complementary contributor is available to support capture and governance. Restructuring, rapid hiring, mergers, and AI adoption can expose weaknesses in these systems; AI is best understood as a revealing force rather than the root cause of fragile knowledge infrastructure.
