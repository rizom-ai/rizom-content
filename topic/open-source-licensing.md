---
title: Open-Source Intellectual Property Governance
---
A governance framework for managing open-source software, shared infrastructure, commercial rights, and brand protection while preserving the software commons. Rizom’s self-hostable platform, portable Markdown data, core runtime, and brains repositories may use AGPL-3.0 to support transparency, client control, and resistance to proprietary capture. The plugin SDK, AT Protocol contracts, and theme interfaces may use Apache-2.0 to enable broad reuse and commercial extensions. Repository notices, contribution terms, metadata, and a Developer Certificate of Origin should clarify contributor rights and maintain clean intellectual-property records through organizational succession.

Rizom B.V. may initially hold the platform copyrights and trademarks, with a planned transfer of core intellectual property and the Rizom brand to an independent foundation or stichting governed as an open commons. The commercial BV should retain a perpetual licence-back, certified practices, partner and service registries, client relationships, and service revenue, separating stewardship of shared infrastructure from the commercial practice without leaving the business commercially hollow. Equity investors therefore receive exposure to services and related commercial activities rather than ownership of the core commons. This structure can strengthen client trust by preserving self-hosting, data ownership, portability, and freedom from vendor lock-in.

Copyright and software licences should operate alongside clear trademark and brand policies. The Rizom name, logo, product names, wordmark, certification marks, domains, package names, and namespaces should be governed through published usage rules, registry controls, and approval requirements. Forks and modified versions should use distinct names and avoid misleading association. Brand protection should begin with clearance searches across EUIPO, TMview, BOIP, company registers, and relevant web sources. A plain RIZOM word-mark filing, initially owned by Rizom B.V. and later assignable to a foundation, generally offers broader protection than a stylized mark. An EU trade mark may suit an international partner ecosystem, with focused specifications covering genuinely used or intended goods and services, potentially in Nice classes 9, 35, 41, and 42. Ongoing governance should address assignments, renewals, genuine-use compliance, opposition monitoring, coexistence arrangements, fallback branding, and potential Madrid Protocol expansion.
