---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for scaling an open platform through a certified network of independent practitioners and institutionally aligned partners. Partners retain their own businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access while delivering under a shared methodology, playbooks, tools, branding, public registry, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities. The framework expands implementation capacity and distribution beyond the core team without requiring proprietary software or creating employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, client ownership, or platform lock-in.

Certification is earned through apprenticeship and co-delivery on real paid engagements, enabling demonstrated capability, practice validation, and case-study development rather than relying solely on a one-time examination. A single Certified Practitioner track uses capability tags and public standing states such as candidate, active, and steward. Only active listed partners may use certification marks, while dormancy may reduce visibility without automatically ending participation. Network participation depends on successful delivery, reference deployments, deal flow, institutional demand, community adoption, and expansion to additional communities.

Governance should preserve shared authorship and legitimate representation, especially where an established intellectual or community network serves as an institutional partner. Partners should co-author the vision, retain meaningful control over how the relationship is represented publicly, and avoid being reduced to a platform-led marketing vehicle. Formal mechanisms may include representation on the foundation or stichting board, a shared founding text, and explicit commitments to the partner’s voice, lineage, and standing. This creates an accountable bridge between established communities and emerging infrastructure while providing clear tests of whether the system is genuinely adopted and transferable.

The framework governs eligibility, agreements, capability tags, engagement records, published commercial terms and price bands, delivery and interoperability standards, evidence sharing, client commitment, accountability, trademark use, revenue allocation, registry publication, disputes, remediation, appeals, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry communicates partner status and capabilities. Partners may remit an agreed share of engagement revenue to the commons—such as a proposed 25% where adopted—to fund platform stewardship. The foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification, registry and trademark rights, listing decisions, standards, enforcement, and institutional representation. Open-source licensing, such as AGPL or a comparable framework, helps prevent proprietary capture, while detailed economics and thresholds remain subject to governance and legal review.
