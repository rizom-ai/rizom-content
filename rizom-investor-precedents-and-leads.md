# Rizom — Investor Precedents and Leads

Checked 7 September 2026. This pass started from comparable companies and traced their actual financing, rather than starting from familiar investor names. No outreach performed. It found eight comparables, seven institutional/strategic additions and eight named individual-backer leads. **No exact precedent was established for Rizom's full combination of independent foundation-owned core IP, operator commercial rights and a contractual 25% commons allocation.**

Separate three dimensions: (1) structural evidence, such as relevant foundation-held assets or named support for a transfer; (2) protocol/product investment evidence; (3) present cheque/stage/jurisdiction eligibility. None establishes the others automatically. All investors' acceptance of Rizom's actual terms remains unverified.

## 1. Element / Matrix — strongest core-software precedent found, with a material warning

Status invested USD 5m in New Vector in January 2018. A USD 8.5m Series A from Notion Capital, firstminute and Dawn followed in 2019. Automattic extended it with USD 4.6m in May 2020. Historical amounts, not current ticket policies.

The Matrix Foundation's November 2023 account states that Element transferred the Synapse and Dendrite projects and copyrights to the Foundation when it formed, and assigned contributions from 2019; individual contributors retained their own copyright. This is real foundation-held core-software/commercial-company evidence, particularly for Automattic's 2020 investment after the stated assignment period began.

However, in 2023 Element forked the projects into company repositories, adopted AGPL for future development and a CLA enabling commercial dual licensing. The Foundation said it lacked resources to continue active development of its versions. Element described the competitive burden of funding most development while other companies monetised the ecosystem.

Conclusion: genuine precedent, not a clean template or proof of permanent assignment/25% acceptance. The later change makes sustainable commons funding and commercial incentives a central diligence question. Do not portray failure of this precise allocation as established; no such allocation was verified.

Sources: https://www2.matrix.org/blog/2018/01/29/status-partners-up-with-new-vector-fueling-decentralised-comms-and-the-matrix-ecosystem/ ; https://element.io/blog/automattic-backs-matrix-investing-4-6m-in-new-vector/ ; https://www2.matrix.org/blog/2023/11/06/future-of-synapse-dendrite/ ; https://element.io/blog/element-to-adopt-agplv3/ ; https://www.notioncapital.com/portfolio/element

## 2. Automattic / WordPress — named support for moving a key asset into a nonprofit

Toni Schneider's April 2006 seed announcement names Polaris/Mike Hirshland, Blacksmith Capital/Phil Black, Radar/Doug Mackenzie and CNET/Shelby Bonnie. Amount not disclosed there. The financing was for hosting infrastructure and financial resilience; avoid importing later estimates as the announcement's claim.

In September 2010 Matt Mullenweg announced transfer of the WordPress trademark from Automattic to the WordPress Foundation. He explicitly credited board/investor/counsel participants including Mike Hirshland, Phil Black, Tony Conrad and Toni Schneider. This is useful person-level evidence of support for a nonprofit asset transfer. It is a trademark precedent, not proof of foundation ownership of every WordPress copyright, and the seed financing preceded the transfer. Separate legal entities do not by themselves prove independent governance; subsequent disputes and current licences were not audited in this pass.

Current roles verified: Phil Black co-founding partner at True Ventures; Toni Schneider partner at True and Automattic CEO during 2006–2014; Mike Hirshland now co-founder/partner at Resolute. Do not describe Resolute as the Automattic investor or erase Blacksmith/Polaris attribution. The lead follows the people, not an invented current-firm investment.

Sources: https://toni.org/2006/04/13/automattic-funding/ ; https://ma.tt/2010/09/wordpress-trademark/ ; https://wordpressfoundation.org/ ; https://www.trueventures.com/team/phil-black ; https://www.trueventures.com/team/toni-schneider ; https://www.resolute.vc/about

## 3. Mapbox / OpenStreetMap — services on a nonprofit-supported commons

Foundry's October 2013 announcement documents leading a USD 10m Mapbox Series A and explicitly identifies OpenStreetMap/open data in its thesis. OSM Foundation is a separate nonprofit supporting project infrastructure, fundraising and governance. Useful evidence that a shared resource need not be enclosed for an API/service business to attract funding. Not proof of foundation ownership of Mapbox's own software, an exclusive operator or a fixed commons revenue allocation. Mapbox already had substantial customer evidence and the round was much larger than Rizom's. Foundry remains historical/referral research; current deployment and cheque fit not verified, so not automatically added as a current prospect.

Sources: https://foundry.vc/blog/2013/10/our-investment-in-mapbox/ ; https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/About

## 4. Acquia / Drupal — commercial services around community software

Dries Buytaert's December 2007 announcement documents USD 7m Series A led by North Bridge Venture Partners with Sigma Partners and O'Reilly AlphaTech Ventures, to build value-added software/network services around Drupal and contribute to the project. The Drupal Association is a supporting nonprofit; its site identifies the Drupal trademark as Dries Buytaert's, not Association-owned. Good operating-model precedent, not the same IP ownership or round size. Historical firms are not assumed active in this mandate in 2026; retained in evidence map rather than promoted into qualified investors.

Sources: https://www.drupal.org/forum/general/news-and-announcements/2007-12-19/acquia-receives-7-million-series-a-financing ; https://dri.es/acquia-raises-7-million-series-a ; https://www.drupal.org/association/about

## 5. Bluesky / AT Protocol — actual protocol financing and named early backers

Bluesky's July 2023 announcement documents USD 8m seed led by Neo and names Joe Beda, Bob Young, Amjad Masad, Amir Shevat, Heather Meeker, Katelyn Donnelly, Brad Fitzpatrick, Automattic and Protocol Labs among others. It explicitly discusses open commons, user ownership, freedom to leave and services-led monetisation without a data moat. This is directly relevant to Rizom's AT Protocol basis.

The issuer was a public benefit C corporation, NOT a commercial operator with a separate foundation holding core IP. PBC status is not an equivalent asset boundary. Historical individual participation does not establish current angel activity, cheque size or conflict clearance.

Source: https://bsky.social/about/blog/7-05-2023-business-plan

## 6. Tana — product precedent, not structural precedent

The February 2025 announcement is a USD 14m Series A led by Tola Capital with follow-on from Lightspeed, Northzone, Alliance VC and firstminute, bringing TOTAL funding to USD 25m. Not a USD 25m seed round. It names Lars Rasmussen among earlier believers/backers and distinguishes new Series A participants. Product relevance: knowledge graphs, living knowledge and human/AI collaboration. No independent-foundation IP structure established. Investor overlap creates potential conflicts to screen.

Source: https://outliner.tana.inc/blog/tana-raises-usd14m-series-a

## 7. Ghost — sustainable hosting example, not sellable-operator equity precedent

Ghost says it is a nonprofit foundation funded by managed hosting; its constitution prevents sale of the company and revenues are reinvested. It demonstrates sustainable open-source economics, NOT the same outside-equity, saleable-B.V. structure. Older Rizom materials describing Ghost as the same foundation-plus-licensed-B.V. arrangement overstate the analogy. Do not repeat that claim. No canonical pitch or strategy notes are changed by this research.

Source: https://ghost.org/about/

## 8. Sharetribe — useful marketplace/stewardship example with different exit constraints

Sharetribe's history says its 2018 steward-ownership transition prevents sale, IPO and shareholder dividends and retains control with its working team. This is not the founder's chosen growth-and-exit profile for Rizom's operator. Use for marketplace/ownership learning, not compatible conventional exit precedent. Historical financing terms would require separate research.

Source: https://www.sharetribe.com/company/
