# What Rizom delivers, and why it matters

What a client gets from Rizom, how it is delivered, and why a working knowledge system is the precondition for innovation rather than a technical exercise.

## What a client gets

Four engagements. Each stands on its own, and each one's result is the next one's starting point. Described by what the client holds afterwards.

- **Team Workshop.** Half a day. The team leaves agreeing, in writing, on who does what, who decides what, and what the AI is for. EUR 1,500 in-house, EUR 750 online.
- **Knowledge Audit.** Three weeks. An independent finding on which people the organisation cannot afford to lose, what each carries, and where its AI tools work without the context they need. Plus a playbook: three to five changes to roles, rituals and tooling, ranked by cost and impact. EUR 5,000 fixed. The fee credits in full toward a build.
- **Build.** A shared memory their team uses daily, for people and AI, captured from where work already happens. Open source, on their own infrastructure or hosted by us, theirs to run without us. EUR 15,000 as launch client, EUR 35,000 standard.
- **Retainer.** The memory stays current as people come and go, with a monthly review of what it holds and where it is thin. EUR 4,000 a month as launch client, EUR 7,000 standard.

## How it is delivered

Interviews, a facilitated session, a document. Six to ten people, one to one, each asked what they know that nobody else does and what goes wrong if they are not there tomorrow. Then half a day in which the team draws its own map: who knows what, whose judgment counts where, how information moves. The playbook records that map and what to change.

Nothing is installed before step three. The client keeps the playbook whether or not they go further. When they do build, the playbook is the specification, and what gets built is open source and runs on their infrastructure. The method is transactive memory systems: forty years of research on how teams share knowledge without everyone knowing everything.

## Why this leads to innovation

New work is mostly recombination of what people in the organisation already know. A team can only recombine what it can see. Where nobody knows who knows what, or whose judgment settles a question, every new initiative routes through the same two or three people, and stalls when they are busy or gone. Where the map exists and is shared, the team takes on new things without waiting for them.

That is the research finding behind the method. Teams with a strong shared map of expertise outperform teams of equal individual talent. The difference is structural, not personal, which is why it can be diagnosed and repaired.

AI is the current test of this. An AI tool is a new team member with none of the map: it does not know who to defer to, what is settled, or what is context. This is why rollouts underdeliver. Organisations experience it as an AI problem. It is the absence of a knowledge system, made visible. That is also why the work can be bought on an AI budget while fixing an organisational problem.

## Why it is not a technical process

The map is made by people, in the room. The fragile part is not the data but the trust: whose judgment is accepted on which questions, and whether that is agreed or merely assumed. No tool produces that. The software's job is narrower: to keep the map current once it exists, so it does not decay the next time someone leaves.

This is also why the entry engagements contain no software at all. A client can stop after the audit with a document and no dependency on us. The build only makes sense once the organisational work has been done.
