# What Rizom delivers, and why it matters

Every organisation runs on knowledge that lives in people rather than in systems: who knows what, whose judgment settles which questions, how information actually moves. It is rarely written down, because it always worked. It stops working when a key person leaves, a team grows fast, or an AI tool is asked to do a job nobody ever specified.

Rizom makes that knowledge visible, then makes it work. This page explains what that means in practice, and why it matters for more than continuity.

## What you get

Four engagements. Each stands on its own, and each one's result is the next one's starting point.

- **Team Workshop.** Half a day, online or on site. Your team leaves agreeing, in writing, on who does what, who decides what, and what the AI is for. Usually the first time anyone has seen it written down.
- **Knowledge Audit.** Three weeks, fixed price. You know, in writing, which people your organisation cannot afford to lose, what each of them carries, and where your AI tools are working without the context they need. Then what to do about it: three to five changes to roles, rituals and tooling, ranked by cost and impact. The playbook is yours to keep.
- **Build.** A shared memory your team uses daily, for people and AI, captured from where work already happens so nobody has to maintain it. Your team asks the memory before they ask the one person who knows, and the answer is there when that person is away. Open source, on your infrastructure or hosted by us, yours to run without us.
- **Retainer.** The memory stays current as people come and go, with a monthly review of what it holds and where it is thin.

## How it works

The audit is interviews, a facilitated session and a document. Six to ten people, one to one, each asked what they know that nobody else does and what goes wrong if they are not there tomorrow. Then half a day in which your team draws its own map: who knows what, whose judgment counts where, how information moves. The playbook records that map and what to change.

Nothing is installed before the build. You keep the playbook whether or not you go further. If you do build, the playbook is the specification, the audit fee counts toward the work, and what gets built runs on your own infrastructure.

The method is transactive memory systems: forty years of research on how teams share knowledge without everyone needing to know everything.

## Why this is about innovation, not just continuity

New work is mostly recombination of what people in your organisation already know. A team can only recombine what it can see. Where nobody knows who knows what, or whose judgment settles a question, every new initiative routes through the same two or three people and stalls when they are busy or gone. Where the map exists and is shared, the team takes on new things without waiting for them.

The research is consistent on this. Teams with a strong shared map of expertise outperform teams of equal individual talent. The difference is structural, not personal, which is why it can be diagnosed and repaired.

AI is the current test. An AI tool is a new team member with none of the map: it does not know who to defer to, what is settled, or what is context. This is why rollouts underdeliver. It looks like an AI problem. It is the absence of a knowledge system, made visible.

## Why it is not a technical exercise

The map is made by people, in the room. The fragile part is not the data but the trust: whose judgment is accepted on which questions, and whether that is agreed or merely assumed. No tool produces that. The software's job is narrower: to keep the map current once it exists, so it does not decay the next time someone leaves. That is also why the audit contains no software at all, and why the build only makes sense once the organisational work has been done.
