---
title: Organizational Knowledge Systems
---
Organizational knowledge systems are the infrastructure, ownership practices, and routines used to capture, organize, govern, discover, share, and apply critical knowledge across teams, platforms, and organizational contexts. Their health depends on clear responsibility, reliable knowledge flows, trust, coordination, and protection against undocumented expertise and excessive concentration of institutional memory in individuals. Assessment should use interviews and organizational mapping to identify actual ownership, dependencies, gaps, and maintenance needs rather than assume an aspirational future state. Restructuring, rapid hiring, mergers, and AI adoption can expose weaknesses in these systems; AI is often a revealing force rather than the root cause of fragile knowledge infrastructure. When records and institutional context are scattered or difficult to search, a focused pilot—such as a project with Paper Tiger—may be more effective than a wholesale platform redesign. The goal is to establish repeatable practices for knowledge discovery, sharing, and use while preserving human agency and organizational context. Explicit ownership is essential, particularly where no complementary contributor exists to support capture and governance, because weak knowledge circulation can undermine otherwise strong governance and organizational design.
