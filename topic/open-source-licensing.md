---
title: Open-Source Intellectual Property Governance
---
A governance framework for keeping organizational-memory infrastructure open, self-hostable, portable, and resistant to proprietary capture while enabling sustainable commercial activity. Rizom’s runtime, platform, and core repositories may use AGPL-3.0, with the plugin SDK, AT Protocol contracts, and theme interfaces potentially using Apache-2.0 to support broad reuse and commercial extensions without surrendering stewardship of shared infrastructure. Portable Markdown data, complete export and re-import, documented ownership deeds, public repositories, contributor terms, security review, and a Developer Certificate of Origin should provide verifiable data sovereignty and clean intellectual-property records.

Rizom B.V. may initially hold the platform’s copyrights and trademarks, with core intellectual property and the Rizom brand ultimately transferred to an independent foundation or stichting governed as an open commons. The commercial BV would retain its services practice, partner and service registries, client relationships, certified practices, hosted operations, related revenue, and a perpetual licence-back to operate on and support the platform. This separates commons stewardship from commercial operations, gives investors exposure to services and ecosystem activity rather than ownership of the core commons, and allows the business to grow without converting the underlying infrastructure into proprietary intellectual property.

Trademark and brand governance should complement software licensing through clearance searches, published usage rules, registry controls, approval requirements, and protection for the Rizom name, logo, product names, certification marks, domains, package names, and namespaces. Forks and modified versions should use distinct names and avoid misleading association. Ongoing administration should address assignments, renewals, genuine-use compliance, opposition monitoring, coexistence arrangements, fallback branding, and potential international registration. Together, these measures support client trust, portability from managed hosting to self-hosting, clean intellectual-property records, and durable freedom from vendor lock-in.
