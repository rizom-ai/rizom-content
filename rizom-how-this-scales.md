# Rizom — How This Scales

*The unleashed version: what the network looks like when composition works. Companion to [[investor-one-pager]] (the practice and the near-term model) and [[rizom-strategic-investor-brief]]. Drafted 2026-08-31.*

## The claim

At scale, Rizom is not a knowledge-tools company. It is composition infrastructure: teams formed from what people actually know and how they actually work.

Every brain holds two things about its owner. Living knowledge — what they know, kept current as a byproduct of real work, so it never rots the way knowledge bases do. And working patterns — how they decide, coordinate, and collaborate. That second half is what nothing else on the market captures: a CV records claims, LinkedIn records connections, a brain records demonstrated knowledge and demonstrated working style. When brains can find each other, teams stop being assembled from org charts, credentials, and interviews — they get composed from evidence, across organisations and geographies. The methodology for that composition is transactive memory systems: forty years of research on why some teams outperform equally talented ones, extended by Rizom to teams that include AI.

The incumbent is not a competitor product. It is the org chart, the staffing agency, and the network of acquaintance.

## The mechanism

Two engines, different physics.

**Engine A — the practice — runs today and seeds the network.** Every engagement (session → pilot → retainer, delivered by the collective, certified partners multiplying it) deploys brains: client teams, collective members, partners. It funds operations, and every deployment is a node.

**Engine B — composition — is what turns nodes into a network.** The TMS layer, built into the platform, composes teams from what the brains hold. Capacity and demand both scale with nodes: every person who joins brings what they can do *and* the work they know about. This is not a hypothesis about whether people want composition — Superconnectors, the founder's matchmaking network, grew past 1,000 members doing this manually. The platform makes it computational, on memory the members own.

## The curve

Every composed engagement splits 60/15/25, and the split is the structure: 60% to the composed team (capacity scales with nodes, not payroll), 25% commons to the foundation (funds the open platform), 15% to the operator — Rizom B.V. — that composed the team and found the work. Plus hosting for every node that prefers not to run infrastructure.

Illustrative arithmetic, not forecast — average composed engagement €50k, teams of three, an active node in one engagement a year, 40% of nodes hosted at €250/month:

- **1,000 active nodes** — the scale Superconnectors reached by hand — is ~€16M/yr flowing through the network: ~€2.5M/yr operator take plus ~€1.2M hosting ARR for the BV, and ~€4M/yr commons funding a real platform team.
- **10,000 active nodes** is ~€165M/yr throughput: ~€25M operator take plus ~€12M hosting ARR.

The operator's costs do not scale with throughput — delivery is the network's 60%, the platform is commons-funded. That is the unleashed shape: marketplace leverage on a substrate nobody can enclose.

## Why now

Three forces arrived together. Living memory became self-maintaining — knowledge capture always died on maintenance, and AI in the flow of daily work removes that cost; this is new, and it is what makes a brain worth owning. Teams already include AI — and TMS is the one body of team research that extends to members who aren't human; hybrid teams need a composition layer, not another chat tool. And work itself is deconstructing — fractional, project-based, cross-organisational — while the infrastructure for composing it still runs on credentials. Open protocols (the brain is built on AT Protocol) make the network commons instead of walled garden.

## Why nobody can enclose it — and what investors own

People put how they think and work into a network only if no company can take it. So the platform IP, the TMS layer, and the protocol live in an independent foundation, AGPL, funded by the commons share. That neutrality is not a concession — it is why nodes join.

Investors own Rizom B.V.: the sole commercial operator, under a perpetual, irrevocable licence that survives change of control. The operator holds the registry — certification is the credibility dimension of TMS held as a commercial asset — the hosting relationship with every node, the client relationships, and the 15% of everything the network composes.

## The role of money

Every euro does one of two things: raises node inflow (sales capacity keeping Engine A closing — every engagement deploys brains) or builds the composition layer (the engineering that makes Engine B real). The round is sized from those two costs over eighteen months, released against one gate that matters: the first team composed across brains, earning its commons.
