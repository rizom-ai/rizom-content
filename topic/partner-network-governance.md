---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A durable partner-certification model governs admission, participation, accountability, and standing across a distributed service network. An independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees the certification marks, methodology, partner agreements, public machine-readable registry, and fair delisting procedures. The registry is authoritative: only currently listed active partners may use certified marks; candidates may identify themselves only as candidates during an active engagement, and mark rights end when status lapses. Disputes follow documented procedures with written reasons, a response or remedy period, and board appeal.

Certification begins through apprenticeship on a real paid engagement rather than an exam. Partners may hold capability tags and progress from candidate to active to steward. In return for access to the mark, registry, methodology, delivery tools, co-delivery and referral opportunities, partners deliver under published standards, uphold client commitments to portability and transparency, protect the marks, contribute evidence and learning to the commons, and remit an agreed commons share. Active standing depends on ongoing contribution and published expectations, with reduced activity potentially lowering visibility without automatic termination.

Certification does not create employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or Core status. Partners retain their independent businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted open-source platform access if they leave. Transparent registry evidence, published expectations, and clear governance preserve trust for partners and clients, while detailed thresholds, economic terms, and delisting standards remain subject to governance.
