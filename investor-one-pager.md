## Page 1 — Living knowledge an organisation actually owns
*Rizom Brains: a commercial practice on an open platform.*

Every organisation loses knowledge. The context behind decisions, the reasoning of a project, the expertise that leaves with people. The tools that promise to fix this are closed platforms, and whatever you put into them becomes the vendor's asset. Rizom Brains works differently. It is an open, self-hostable brain that an organisation fully owns: plain markdown on their own infrastructure, built on the AT Protocol, licensed under AGPL so nobody can turn it into a proprietary product.

We sell the work, not the software. We deploy and run brains for organisations, and we are building a certified partner network so others can deliver that work too. The platform is open; the practice, the brand and the network are the business.

### What we sell
- We deploy and run brains for organisations, on an engagement that runs from survey and workshop to playbook, pilot and retainer.
- A certified partner network with an open methodology and a public registry, so independent practitioners can deliver the same engagements. No lock-in for them either.

### Traction
- Three pilots in progress, in different sectors: an AI ecosystem, cultural archives, and farming cooperatives in transition to regenerative practice. Each has a tailored proposal on the table.
- Anchor engagement priced and scoped: EUR 3–5k/month retainer plus a EUR 10–20k build.
- The platform is live, self-hostable and open source under AGPL.

### The model — 60/25/15
Every engagement is split 60% delivery, 15% acquisition, 25% commons. The commons share funds the platform and the foundation that stewards it. Current prices are early-adopter prices; standard pricing is roughly double (EUR 35k builds, EUR 7k retainers).

### The ask
A bridge of roughly EUR 75k, covering twelve months: enough to convert the three pilots into recurring retainers, bring in a dedicated closer, and absorb slippage in the ramp. This is not a growth round. It is a bridge to a self-sustaining services business, structured to suit: convertible, revenue-based or SAFE.
- Use of funds: founder runway during the ramp, a first commercial hire, pilot delivery costs.
- Break-even: at current prices, one retainer plus one small engagement a month covers the business.

### What your equity buys
The platform IP and the Rizom trademark are held by Rizom B.V. today and move to an independent foundation once it is founded; contributor sign-offs (DCO, to "Rizom B.V. and its successors and assigns") make that transfer a single recorded deed. That is why clients can trust there is no lock-in. Rizom B.V., the entity you invest in, keeps the commercial practice, the partner registry, the client relationships and an exclusive commercial licence back to the platform — perpetual, irrevocable, and surviving any change of control. You are backing the company that operates on top of the open platform, not a proprietary software bet.

### In parallel — non-dilutive
We are also pursuing non-dilutive funding for the platform itself: an NLnet/NGI Zero application (EUR 50k), WBSO, and cloud credits. Investor capital funds the business, not the infrastructure.

## Page 2 — How this grows: three revenue lines

Rizom does not scale by the founder billing more hours. Three revenue lines switch on in sequence, all built on the same open platform. Engagements produce case studies. Case studies certify partners. Partners spread the platform, and every deployment is a candidate for hosting. Hosting margin funds the platform, which in turn strengthens the practice and the partners.

### 01 — The practice (the proof, not the growth)
Direct delivery is capped by founder time at roughly EUR 150–250k a year: a healthy practice, not a company. Its job is to fund the business today and to train the first partners. Every certification happens on a real, paid engagement, delivered together.

### 02 — The partner network
Certified partners deliver under the Rizom name and methodology, and 25% of each engagement goes to the commons. That share carries no delivery cost for Rizom. A modestly active partner, doing two builds and two retainers a year at standard pricing, brings in around EUR 60k a year. Partners get the trademark, the registry listing, leads and the methodology, and keep everything except the name if they ever leave.
- Prove it: 5 partners → ~EUR 300k commons flow/yr (+ ~EUR 50k certification fees)
- Real network: 20 partners → ~EUR 1.2M/yr (+ ~EUR 150k)
- Category: 50 partners → ~EUR 3M/yr (+ ~EUR 300k)

### 03 — Hosted brains
Self-hosting stays free. That is the basis of the trust, and it does not change. But most organisations prefer not to run infrastructure themselves, so "operated by Rizom" offers managed hosting of the same platform, with export to self-hosting at any time. Rizom already hosts trial brains at its own cost; pricing them turns an expense into margin. At EUR 150–500 per brain per month, 100 brains is roughly EUR 360k ARR; 500 is EUR 1.8M. Every practice or partner deployment is a hosting lead that costs nothing to acquire.

Ghost has run this model for over a decade: a non-profit foundation owns the open-source IP, and its hosted service funds the whole organisation, without venture capital. Rizom's foundation plus licensed BV follows the same structure.

### Where this could fail
- If nobody wants the certification, line 02 stalls. Test: a first certified partner within twelve months.
- If organisations prefer to self-host, line 03 stays small. Test: the share of pilots that choose managed hosting.
- If engagements do not close, that is a sales problem, not a model problem. It is what the bridge pays to fix.

### What this means for the bridge
- The EUR 75k carries line 01 to self-sufficiency, which is the precondition for 02 and 03.
- Institutional capital becomes realistic once partners are certified and hosted revenue is visible: numbers a fund can underwrite.
- It may also never be needed. On the Ghost path, lines 02 and 03 fund everything. Both options stay open.
