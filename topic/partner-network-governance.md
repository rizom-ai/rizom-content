---
title: Partner Certification and Network Governance
---
A governance and operating framework for scaling delivery through a certified network of independent practitioners. Partners use shared open-platform infrastructure, methodology, delivery tools, brand, referrals, and co-delivery opportunities to provide structured organisational services such as surveying, workshops, playbooks, pilots, and retainers, expanding implementation capacity beyond founder-led delivery while preserving consistency across client engagements.

The framework defines eligibility, formal agreements, apprenticeship-based certification through real paid engagements, capability tags, progression from candidate to active partner to steward, participation standards, engagement records, accountability, and fair delisting. A public, machine-readable registry is the authoritative record of partner status: only active listed partners may use certification marks, while candidates may identify themselves as candidates during an active engagement. Mark rights end when status lapses. Partners retain independent businesses, capabilities, infrastructure, and self-hosted access to the open platform; certification creates no employment, exclusivity, guaranteed work, permanent standing, ownership, or Core status.

In exchange for participation, partners uphold published delivery, transparency, portability, client-commitment, evidence-sharing, and brand-protection standards and remit any agreed commons share. Ongoing contribution and published expectations determine standing, although reduced activity may lower visibility without automatically ending participation. The registry, brand, and methodology may support the commercial network’s value proposition while an independent foundation or designated marks holder oversees certification methods, agreements, public policies, registry and pricing information, engagement records, and fair enforcement. Disputes require documented procedures, written reasons, an opportunity to respond or remedy, and board appeal. Legal review and established registry and approval practices should inform implementation, while detailed thresholds, economics, eligibility rules, and delisting standards remain subject to governance.
