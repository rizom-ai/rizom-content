# Town Hall Outline — The Knowledge Session

*Internal. Speaker outline for the Rizom Town Hall of 9 September 2026, 60 minutes on Discord voice. Companion notes: [[tms-services-plan]], [[the-knowledge-session-one-pager]].*

## 0. Open (5 min)

Why this hour.
- The Knowledge Session is what pays for the next months. One session roughly covers a month. The first one needs to close in about eight weeks.
- It funds the year; it is not the year. Product and pilots stay the priority. This is the smallest thing a warm contact can say yes to.
- Three things today: how it works, run part of it on ourselves, ask for help on the two halves that aren't built yet.

## 1. The reframe (5 min)

- Every organisation runs on knowledge that lives in people: who knows what, whose judgment settles which questions, how information actually moves.
- Nobody writes it down because it always worked.
- It stops working when a key person leaves, a team grows fast, or an AI tool is asked to do a job nobody ever specified.
- AI didn't break the knowledge system. It exposed that there never was one.
- That's why this can be bought on an AI budget while fixing an organisational problem.

## 2. How it runs (15 min)

Three steps, three weeks, fixed price EUR 5,000.

**Interviews** — six to ten people, one to one, 45 minutes.
- Two questions: what do you know that nobody else does, and what breaks if you're not here tomorrow.
- Status: not built. The protocol needs writing. Most of it can be derived from the workshop exercises.

**Workshop** — half a day with the team, on site or online.
- The team draws its own map: who knows what, whose judgment counts where, how information moves. Everyone sees the same picture, usually for the first time.
- Status: designed and ready. Half-day in-house up to 15, or 90 min online.

**Playbook** — the map as it is today, plus three to five interventions ranked by cost and impact.
- Three dimensions: expertise, trust, coordination.
- Status: not built. Needs a template.

What the buyer gives: six to ten names and half a day.
What the buyer keeps: a document. Nothing to install, no dependency on us. If they build later, the playbook is the spec, the fee counts toward it, and what gets built is open source on their own infrastructure.

Who buys: whoever owns how the work flows. Director, head of operations, programme lead. Institutions and small teams carrying a lot of knowledge.

Triggers to listen for: a key person resigning, a reorg or merger, rapid hiring, an AI rollout that underdelivered.

## 3. Run it on Rizom (20 min)

Rules up front:
- One person at a time. Everyone else only listens. No cross-talk until everyone has answered.
- People talk, I type. Nobody else writes. I put each answer into the shared doc as it comes, under one of three columns: expertise / trust / coordination.
- I answer last.

The two questions, to each person in turn:
1. What do you know about Rizom that nobody else does?
2. What breaks if you're gone tomorrow?

After the round:
- Read the doc back to the room. Point at the thinnest spots on the map. Don't fix them now.
- Name what we just made: our own fragility test, and the first draft of the playbook template.

## 4. The thinking (5 min)

Only if there's time and energy. Otherwise skip to 5.
- Transactive memory systems: forty years of research on how teams share knowledge without everyone knowing everything.
- Three dimensions: specialisation (who knows what), credibility (whose judgment is trusted), coordination (how it moves).
- Research puts high-performing teams above 70% on TMS measures; most teams start well below. The gap is structural, not personal.
- AI is a new team member with none of the three. That's the extension.

## 5. The ask (10 min)

Three things, take names for each:
1. Who works on the interview protocol.
2. Who works on the playbook template, starting from today's map.
3. Who knows someone matching the triggers. Names, not categories.

Close:
- Today's map goes into the rizom-ai brain, not Discord.
- Next town hall: protocol and template drafts, and where the first conversations stand.
