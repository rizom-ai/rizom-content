---
title: Open-Source Intellectual Property Governance
---
A governance framework for managing the software commons, commercial rights, and brand protection in an open-source ecosystem. Core runtime and brains repositories may use AGPL-3.0, while the plugin SDK, AT Protocol contracts, and theme interfaces use Apache-2.0 to support broad reuse and commercial extensions. Repository metadata, README notices, contribution terms, and a Developer Certificate of Origin should clarify contributor rights and preserve clean intellectual-property management through organizational succession.

Rizom B.V. may initially hold copyrights and trademarks, with a planned transfer of core intellectual property and the Rizom brand to an independent foundation or stichting. The commercial BV should retain a perpetual licence-back, certified practices, partner and service registries, client relationships, and service revenue so the transfer does not leave it commercially hollow. Equity investors therefore receive exposure to the commercial practice rather than ownership of the core commons.

Copyright and trademark rights operate alongside software licences. The Rizom name, logo, product names, wordmark, certification marks, domains, package names, and namespaces should be governed through published policies, registry controls, and approval requirements. Forks and modified versions must use distinct names and avoid misleading association.

Brand protection should begin with clearance searches across EUIPO, TMview, BOIP, company registers, and broader web sources, including potentially conflicting marks in software-related fields. A plain RIZOM word-mark filing, initially owned by Rizom B.V. and later assignable to a stichting, generally provides broader protection than a stylized mark. An EU trade mark is suitable for an international partner ecosystem; a Benelux filing may establish Paris Convention priority if followed by an EU application within six months. Specifications should cover only genuinely used or intended goods and services, potentially including software, AI and knowledge-management tools, SaaS, software development, consultancy, education, and workshops in Nice classes 9, 35, 41, and 42. Risk can be managed through focused specifications, carve-outs, coexistence agreements, fallback composite branding, opposition monitoring, and clear procedures for assignment, renewal, genuine-use compliance, and Madrid Protocol expansion.
