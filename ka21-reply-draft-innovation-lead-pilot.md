Dear Helmut, dear Wolfgang,

Thank you for the good conversation — and for the sharp questions. Here is the written summary you asked for, along your five points. Choosing a new use case in a young professional field with high fluctuation is a smart starting point: high turnover is exactly where organisational memory breaks, so the value of a Brain shows up fast.

## 1. Workflow — the six months

The engagement runs in three phases:

- **Month 1 — Diagnosis.** We start with a Knowledge Session: individual interviews with 6–10 people from the target field (the "fragility test": what does this person know that nobody else does, and what breaks when they leave?), a half-day workshop, and a playbook that maps the expertise, trust boundaries and coordination patterns of the domain. This becomes the specification for the application. Your part: the concept, access to interviewees, domain knowledge. Our part: method, facilitation, synthesis.
- **Months 2–4 — Co-development.** We build the Brain and its agent in short iterative cycles (roughly bi-weekly). A working version exists early — from about month two you are testing a live system, not reviewing mock-ups. Your part: feedback, real content, domain corrections. Our part: all engineering.
- **Months 5–6 — Hardening and handover.** Validation with a real test user, documentation, deployment setup, and training your team so you can operate and demonstrate it yourselves.

## 2. What you have in hand after six months

A working, self-contained application: an organisational Brain with an acting agent for the chosen field — it captures knowledge, answers questions with the organisation's own context, and stays current as people come and go. Concretely:

- The platform is **open source (AGPL)** and self-hostable; all content lives as plain, portable markdown. You own the deployment and every byte in it — there is no lock-in by design.
- Handover includes deployment documentation, an operations guide, and the playbook from phase one.
- On the "like WordPress" question: **that is how it works today** — in the wordpress.org sense: open-source software you install and run on your own infrastructure, at no licence cost, rather than a locked hosted service. Part of the handover is training your team to stand up an instance for a customer yourselves, and the packaging gets smoother with every deployment — yours included.

## 3. What KA21 provides

- The use-case concept and domain access (interviewees, sample content).
- Availability, phase-shaped rather than flat: **month 1 is the substantial ask** — roughly 2–3 days in total across the month, for arranging and sitting the interviews plus the half-day workshop. From month 2 onward it is one fixed weekly touchpoint of 60–90 minutes plus light asynchronous feedback — call it 2–4 hours per week.
- A fictional customer is enough to build against for the first months, but we would want **one real test user in the final two months** — validation against reality is what turns the pilot into a sellable reference. It does not have to be a paying customer.
- Budget: see point 4. No infrastructure needed on your side during development; the pilot instance runs on our infrastructure, included in the fee, and is handed over at the end.

## 4. Costs

- **Pilot build: EUR 15,000 fixed** for the six months. That is our early-adopter rate — standard pricing is EUR 35,000 — and we offer it in exchange for what makes you an Innovation Lead rather than a customer: co-delivery, and the right to use the result as a public reference case.
- Invoicing is staged: **EUR 5,000 at the start** (the Knowledge Session, which stands on its own and gives you a natural go/no-go point before the build), with the remainder in milestone tranches across the build.
- **Afterwards, the result is freely usable — no licence fees, ever.** The software is open source; running it costs you only your own infrastructure.
- Optionally, a **retainer (EUR 4,000/month early-adopter)** covers managed hosting, upgrades and support if you prefer not to operate it yourselves. Entirely optional and cancellable — you can always take the exported system and self-host.

## 5. How KA21 earns money with this

This is where "Innovation Lead" means more than being a customer. Rizom scales through **certified partners**, not through our own payroll — and certification happens exactly the way this pilot is structured: by co-delivering a real engagement with us. So the pilot is simultaneously your certification path.

As a certified partner, KA21 can then sell and deliver Brain engagements to similar organisations under the Rizom name and methodology. The economics per engagement: **60% delivery, 15% acquisition, 25% commons** (the commons share funds the open platform). Where you both bring and deliver a deal, KA21 keeps 75%. At standard pricing, a partner delivering two builds and two retainers a year generates roughly EUR 240,000 in engagement revenue, of which the partner keeps three quarters. You keep your own firm, clients and everything you build — if you ever leave the network, you lose only the name.

On the reusable building block: everything domain-specific we develop in your pilot (the field's knowledge model, agent behaviours, templates) becomes the toolkit *you* deploy for the next customers in that vertical — as the certified partner who co-created it, with the reference case carrying your name. The plugin layer is Apache-licensed precisely so partners can build and commercially exploit extensions on top. There is no passive royalty on the module itself — the participation model is that you are first and fastest in the vertical you opened, with the margin structure above.

Happy to walk through any of this in a follow-up call — and if you want a low-risk way to start, the Knowledge Session is designed to be exactly that: a bounded first step that produces standalone value before you commit to the full build.
